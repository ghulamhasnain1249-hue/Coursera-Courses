In practice, and as it has been assimilated
into the collective memory, the Black Shabat – the British action against
the leadership of the Jewish Yishuv put an end to the rebellion of the Jewish Yishuv. To what extent has this action influenced
history in the long term? I think that that what the British did
had a significant influence I don't really know what the Black Shabat
is really, as it is a Zionist terminology Operation Agatha started on June 29th 1946
and ended two weeks later, on July 11th. I think that the effect of what happened there
lies essentially in what has not happened. I'll try to explain this: First of all,
what is the rebellion movement of the Jewish Yishuv? In fact there was no such movement.
In 1945, the Jewish Agency was in great distress, the distress of a political organization which
tried to experience history for a second time. If after the First World War they received the Balfour Declaration, ,they had a much greater case after the colossal tragedy in Europe.
They really thought that the establishment of the Jewish State was around the corner. In addition, in the summer of 1945 there was also a general
election in England, and everyone there was pro-Zionist. And then, nothing happened. The Labor party won the elections,
and they had a different agenda,
an agenda of welfare state, ending the empire. And the first thing they did, as is normal, they studied the issue, it wasn't going anywhere, and when you are in euphoria,
bringing your attention to such things is difficult. This is what led the Jewish Agency, which was on the
brink of inability to govern their own people, which to that point was their greatest force -
The National Authority. This is when the Jewish Agency declared itself as the
rebellion movement of the Jewish Yishuv, so that it could act on both channels: the official channel, as an existing body as per paragraph
4 of the British Mandate and which gave it a legal status, and on the unofficial channel. Ben Gurion already said it:
"if I won't fight them, they will fight me," and he meant his own people. -	Meaning the dissenters?
-	No, not the dissenters, they were marginal. I mean the public, the Jewish public in Israel and the
Jewish population around the world which wouldn't
have accepted any policy that didn't include a form of struggle. This was the first time in the history of Zionism that they launched
a struggle which was mostly illegal. There had been sort of illegal struggles before then e.g. the illegal immigration in 1939, but now they were
talking about a real struggle, an almost military struggle. Even though it was a limited military struggle,
rather symbolic actions, on trains or bridges, the sort of actions that served to showcase that they can do it, actions in which the weak side tries to convince the strong
side through guerilla action, not terror, rather symbolic guerilla actions. And by that same opportunity, they figured why not
coordinate their actions with the dissenters. This was not a joint movement, or a united movement,
it was an attempt to take control over the dissenters, which was a partial success. -	So why did the dissenters split away?
-	I think that the dissenters wanted more than just weapons and food, which they were lacking. They wanted legitimation. And this gave them legitimacy to some extent,
as their modus operandum got accepted in a way. But the agreement between the two was limited. It was limited to actions that were accepted by the dissenters. They had agreed on purchases, even of violent means,
such as weapons and the dissenters would continue their own way. And the rest would be done in agreement,
and that is important for the continuation of our story, as on the other end of this was the British High Commissioner
who understood that the greatest asset of the Jewish settlement was its National Authority. Therefore he decided to momentarily hurt that National
Authority, to force the Jewish Agency to a) fight its own terror cells, i.e. the dissenters, and
b) agree to the division of the state as we know the Jewish Agency had accepted the division of
the state in 37, but then retrieved its agreement in 42,
in favor of the Greater Israel, sort of. This was done by the arrest of the legal Jewish leadership and the members of the Palmach, which were the military wing of the Hagana. The British High Commissioner Alan Cunningham seperated on his own, he had Montgomery's army on the one hand, and the Jewish Yishuv on the other the army could have crushed the Jewish Yishuv quite easily, if only they had wanted to. This limited action – because this really was a limited action –
which caused barely any casualties, and only selective arrests, they arrested all the leaders,
aside from Golda Meir, who by the way got offended by
the fact that she was not arrested. Neither did they arrest Chaim Weizman, because he was Chaim Weizman, and he was British. This was enough for the Zionist leadership to get the hint,
and a month later they really did declare the end of the Jewish Yishuv Rebellion. Between Operation Agatha and the end of the Jewish Yishuv Rebellion,
occurred the attack on the King David Hotel, on July 22. -	Which belonged to the rebellion movement or not?
-	I think it did. Here we must clarify what the role of the Etzel was.
If there is anything I would hold against the Etzel it is that they
take pride in this attack up until today. But in practice it was probably just a part of the agreement of the Coordination Committee. However, even within the rebellion movement, meaning within the
Jewish Agency there was a dispute, to stop or not to stop. And this action fell through the cracks, which does not legitimize this action,
but the Etzel was definitely not alone in it. In the long term, as I said, the fact that the British Mandate has
not crushed the Jewish Yishuv – and for this we have historical proofs means that they have actually saved the Jewish Yishuv from itself, in a way. -	What does that mean?
-	This means that the Hagana was in fact neutralized during the rebellion, the Palmach which was the army of the Hagana turned into some kind of guerilla force, which acted almost as a terror-like organization, had almost been dismantled. But the worth of this organization was fully recognized two
years later, in the war of independence. The dismantlement was prevented. But more importantly,
what had been prevented was an unnecessary struggle against Britain, which would have eradicated all the achievements of the 30s and the period of WWII. Thank you so much
