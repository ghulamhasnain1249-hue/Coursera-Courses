This Hebrew culture goes side by side
with the transplanted European culture. Tel Aviv for example,
turned into a modern Bauhaus, white city, merging European and
Mediterranean architectural styles. The houses in rural
areas had red roofs and adopted a European rather than
an Arab style of building. Cultural institutions, such as
the Hebrew University in Jerusalem, the Philharmonic Orchestra in Tel Aviv,
the Tel Aviv art museum, the first international Jewish Olympic
games known as Maccabiah, were initiated during the 20s and the 30s. The European backgrounds of most
immigrants gave this cultural expression a European flavor
mixed with an Oriental influence. Folk dance was the epitome
of such a mixture. During the 30s the leadership
provided general vision and guidance according to these principles. 1, The aim of the Zionist project is to
reach a Jewish majority in Palestine. 2, Until the of such a goal, the Zionist movement should
concentrate on practical agenda. Purchasing new land, encouraging more
immigration, raising more money for national endeavors, building more
institutions and social organizations. 3, There is no need to declare that
the final goal is to establish a state for the Jews in Palestine. It is better to concentrate it on
building a viable society on this land. 4, The Arabs were perceived as local
population that lived in this area, and could remain there. However, they were not seen as partner in
the process of building a new society. 5, Great Britain in general was
perceived as partner of the Zionist project despite some dispute and
disagreement with each diplomatic measure. 6, The socialist and collectivist
market economy seems preferable for creating a healthy economy and
a just society. Though private property and private
capital can be used to achieve this end. From the 20s, and
especially during the 30s, the right-wing composition headed by
Jabotinsky challenged these assumptions. He formed the Revisionist party and later
established a new Zionist organization that opposed the establishment. He thought that Zionists
should openly declare that their aim is to establish a Jewish
state on both sides of the Jordan River according to the original
map of the British mandate. He argued the Zionists in Great Britain
had to form an iron wall of a joined military force against the Arab that
will never comply to the Jewish program. He also rejected the socialist
inclination of labor movement and advocate a more liberal,
free market economy. On the other hand of the spectrum, certain Jewish intellectuals
form the Peace Alliance, arguing that Arabs should be treated as
equal partner in the forthcoming state, striving for one bi-national Jewish-Arab
state in the whole mandatory area. Both alternatives did not really challenge
the leadership of the organization. Zhabotinsky was supported by
less than 10% of the population. Though in Europe he had more
than 35% of the Jewish support. The Peace Alliance had no appeal to most
Jews, and even moderate Arabs rejected its position, since they could not accept any
Jewish sovereign existence in the area. Indeed, the conflict with
the Palestinian Arabs intensified during the decade of the 30s. Culminating in the Arab revolt
against British colonialism and Zionism in 1936 up to 1939.
