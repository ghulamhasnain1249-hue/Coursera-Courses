As for the struggle against the
White Paper, it was confined solely to operation of illegal immigration
continued land purchasing and building illegal settlement. The Zionists attempted to help European Jews
who were trying to flee, but were refused to entrance by the British. During the war, 24 vessels
dangerously overloaded with refugees with minimal quantity of food and
water embarked toward Palestine. Most of them were captured by
the British Coast Guard and were sent to detention camps. Some 10,000 illegal immigrants
managed to reach the land. The authorities permitted
the entry only in deduction of the immigration certificates issued to
the Jews according to the white paper. More than 1,200 of these
refugees drowned en route. The struggle for settlements and pave new way of buying land despite
the law that restricted such operations. During World War II, 45 new
settlements were established in remote places such as the far Galilee,
the Dead Sea and the South. It coalesced with the Zionist policy of
creating a territorial continuity and expanding the area of future Jewish
state in the event of a partition. Indeed, the war and
the Holocaust changed the Zionist policy. Ideologically, the Holocaust
gave a tragic justification to the Zionist argument,
the Jews have no future in the diaspora. Most to the them claim that assimilation
would put an end to the Jewish existence. The Holocaust show that physical
annihilation was an even greater threat. That almost put an end to Jewish
existence in Europe altogether. Politically, the destruction
of the European Jury left the Zionist movement as an avant-garde
movement without followers. The hope that the Zionist community
could pave the way to a Jewish mass immigration that would ultimately turn
the Jews in the land of Israel into a majority was proven false. There were no more millions of Jews,
they evaporated in Auschwitz. As a result of this realization, the
leader of the Zionist movement convened in the Biltmore Hotel in New York in
1942 and adopted the Biltmore Program. They demanded the opening of Palestine
to free Jewish immigration and the Jewish agency would get a charter
to establish a Jewish commonwealth in the area as a part of the new democratic
world order that would follow the war. This announcement practically called for
the creation of a sovereign state for the Jews within certain borders where
the Jews would be the majority. It meant that they accepted and even advocated the geographical
partition of mandatory Palestine.
