- With the outbreak of WWII,
Ben Gurion made his famous statement: "We will fight against Britain as though there is no war,
and we will fight with Britain as though there was no white book." - Yes, what he said before the Mapai directory on
July 12, 1939, two weeks after the war started, he said what Shabtai Tevet (who became his biographer)
called the 'double formula': "we will fight against the white book as though Hitler didn't exist,
and we will fight against Hitler as though there was no white book." Now this is a sentence as you would find it
in political sciences, meaning this is theory. How do you implement such a sentence? One thing I want to say first of all,
when a leader can say such a sentence to his public, it says a lot about the leader,
but it also says a lot about his public. This is a politically savvy public, which is
able to understand complex statements. Usually, a leader speaks to his audience in black and white
statements, good vs. bad, with us or against us. But Ben Gurion said something very complex: how do you fight the British policy and how do you
fight against the absolute evil that was Hitler. - In a way that is one thing and its opposite. -Yes, exactly. This was the message that was
said to the entire Jewish public. Also Etzel joined this strategy, and this is why
it ended up falling apart, and Lehi came out of it. But by and large the Jewish public accepted this idea,
and as I said this says something about the public. In practice, how do you implement such a thing? There's the famous story of the members of
"Alyia Bet"(Illegal immigration), in 1939, who received an order from Israel, in the framework
of the cooperation with the British mandate, to drown a ship that was at their
disposal in the estuary of the Danube as part of the effort of the British forces to prevent
the advancement of Germany and its allies in the Black Sea. But there were 800 refugees waiting on the shore and who
knew what would become of them, so what does one do? Do you drown the boat or do you take those refugees to fight
the policy of the White Book and bring them to Eretz Israel? In this case they decided not to drown the boat.
But this is the kind of dilemma that the Yishuv
had to deal with all the time. By and large, their policy was to first and
foremost fight Hitler, there's no doubt about it. They didn't abandon their own policy, and the British
during WWII have not just supported the Yishuv, almost in every aspect, but also established a
committee to give a second thought to the whole
issue whether the White Book policy i.e. a bi-national state would be the right solution,
and this committee's proclivity was to return to the
partition principle from 1937. But because of internal British problems, and the
assassination of Lord Moyne in Cairo by a member of
Lehi in 1944, this whole business was shelved. By and large, this policy, which the
British didn't like, has not disturbed the Zionist movement,
the Yishuv, and Britain, in spite of their disagreements,
to grow much closer even on a practical level, than they were during the days of the
Arab rebellion when the cooperation was
declared and very clear. - How did this rapprochement during the war
express itself on a practical level? - I'll give you one illustrative example
so as not to speak in theoretical terms only. The Jewish faculty established in those
days hosting committees whose responsibility it was to host
military troops from Britain,
New Zealand, Australia and India. Families hosted soldiers in their homes
and fed them, they established clubs
for the British soldiers, they sent young girls to their balls,
it didn't end there, but this was part of the deal. There was a whole project of embracing
the British presence which grew here,
even more so as the war was underway, because Palestine had become a logistical hub,
a holiday destination, a center for Britain. And what they found in the Jewish Yishuv
was not something they could find with the Arabs, with whom it wasn't clear where they were at,
also there wasn't the necessary figure among
the Arabs that could say how things would be done. What is fascinating is that this whole
rapprochement project was eliminated
from the collective memory. What is difficult for us to assimilate is
that since 1942, since Britain removed the
threat impending upon the Land of Israel, the political discord increased, but the
economic situation here was not good,
it was excellent. And this is quite difficult to remember,
because what we do remember is what happened
at the exact same time 1942-1945 in Europe. - And this is the irony. Thank you very much.
