Already at the beginning of the war, when Zionist leader postponed
any potential confrontation with Great Britain, few Irgun fighters left
the organization and formed the Lechi. Under the leadership of Abraham Stern,
known as Yair, they vowed to fight British empire and even thought to collaborate
with the Axis power for this purpose. Lehi was a small clandestine extremis
organization determined to fight for liberation by any means necessary,
and up to the bitter end. Its impact, however,
was insignificant during the war years, especially after the assassination
of Stern by the British. Early in 1944,
when German defeat seems approaching, the Irgun cease to
collaborate with the British. Under the leadership
of the Menachem Begin, who headed the Revisionist Movement,
following Jabotinsky's death. The Irgun joined the Lehi in revolt
against the British administration in Palestine. Few hundred members of Lehi and
Irgun defied the policy of the official leaders of the Yeshuv
and the Zionist movement, and planed attacks on British targets. In doing so,they hoped to launch
a massive anti-British resistance movement that would force Great
Britain to change its policy or get out of Palestine altogether. These underground organizations that
waged war against British authority were termed dissidents because
they rejected the authority of the Yeshuv‘s political leadership with
their violent operations. The assassination in Cairo of Lord Moyne,
the British minister for the Middle East By two members of Lehi,
infuriated the Zionist the leadership. Thus began at the end of 1944,
an operation known as the Saison, during which a member of the Haganah rounded up
members of the dissident organizations. They held them. In improvised detention
facilities in order to stop them from further anti-british action. In some extreme cases, member of the dissident's groups
were handed over to the British. These actions could lead to violent
reactions on the part of the dissidents. Begin, the head of the Irgun, ordered his member not to resist,
thereby prevented an internal fight. In the end the operation
against the organization proved counterproductive and were stopped. When the war ended,
the British Labor Party took over Britain. During the parties term in a position,
it had drafted a pro-Zionist platform. However, when it rose to power,
the new Labor government adopted the principal set forth
in the White papers of 1939. This post war policy was extremely
frustrating for the Zionists. Consequently, Ben Gurion decided
against Weizmann's opinion to establish the Hebrew Resistance
Movement in November 1945. Members of the Haganah, Irgun, and
Lechi consolidated an effort based on the agreement that they will fully
obey the Yishuv's political leadership. That would have to approve any military
operation against Great Britain. Thus, the leadership of
the Yishuv exerted its authority over the descendants while
they in return obtain legitimacy. The Hebrew Resistance Movement carried
out diverse military operations, while trying to minimize
unnecessary casualties. They liberated illegal
immigration from detention camps blew up a British radar which
was used to detect incoming ships. Blew up railway tracks, and
attacked police station and armed vehicles. In June 1946,
Palamach units blew up 11 bridges. That connected Palestine with
its neighboring countries in what is known as
the night of the bridges. The British responded aggressively in what known as Black Saturday. They blocked communication lines,
imposed siege on major cities. Captured the building of the Jewish
agencies in Jerusalem, and arrested many political leaders. The army and police forces searched for
illegal arms in various places. They discovered a huge cache of arms and
confiscated it, a great deal of weaponry. This reaction rekindled the debate whether the armed struggle against
the British is worth anyway. Weizmann viewed it as an
obstacle to his political and diplomatic effort to
change British policy. He used his influence to put
an end to the military struggle. Ben-Gurion ordered the commander of
the Hagana to cease the resistance operation. The Irgun waited for a few days, and then blew up
part of the King David Hotel in Jerusalem, which served as the British
government's headquarter in Palestine. 91 people were killed in this blast. Among them, British, Arabs, and Jews. After this operation, the Zionist leadership withdrew from
any comprehensive military struggle. The Hebrew Resistance Movement
was officially dismantled.
