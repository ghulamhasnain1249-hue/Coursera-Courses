The organized Yeshuv returned to a more
limited struggle focusing on nonviolent resistance against white paper British
policy through illegal immigration and settlements. The Irgun and Lehci however continued
fighting against the British, becoming more and more extremist and
increasingly taking on a terrorist cast of murdering British officers and
exploding bomb that killed civilians. Some of the members were captured,
put to trial, expelled, arrested, and even executed by the British. The dissidents believed that only such a war of attrition would make
the British leave Palestine and would ultimately lead to the establishment
of the Jewish state in the land of Israel. Yet the leadership of the Zionist
movement thought that diplomacy, nonviolent resistance and moral persuasion were better means to force Great Britain
to change its policy and leave. Focusing the effort on illegal
immigration they hired ship in Europe and loaded them with thousands of would-be
immigrants, survivors of the Holocaust. 65 ships carrying about 70,000
refugees attempted to enter Palestine along 1946 and 1947. About 4,000, 5,000 of them managed
to reach the shore, but the vast majority were caught by the British and
sent to detention camps in Cyprus. Illegal immigration became a powerful
diplomatic tool against the British. Blocking the entrance of Jewish Holocaust
refugees to their aspired home in the land of Israel undermined the
mole authority of the mandatory regime. Confrontations between
British soldiers and ragged immigrants cast a negative image of
Britain in the arena of public opinion. The most famous incident was
the capture of the ship Exodus in July 1947 with 4500
immigrants on board. During the struggle, several
immigrants were killed and wounded. The ship was sent back to Europe and
the passengers went on hunger strikes. World public opinion railed against the
abuse, whereby thousands of unfortunate refugees would be returned to
displaced persons camps in Germany. The Zionists won the battle
of world public opinion. In 1947, Great Britain offered to
hand over the issue of Palestine to the United Nations. Some viewed this move as evidence that
Britain was tired of its colonial responsibilities, and like in other places
wanted to reduce the size of the empire. Others saw it as a British exhaustion resulting
primarily from the Zionist struggle. On the other hand, there are historians
that argued that Britain wanted to reinforce its position in Palestine by
obtaining United Nation's support and especially that of the USA for
the continuation of the mandate. Anyhow, this move started
the historical process of ending British control over Palestine. What were to come in it's
place was still unknown.
