Welcome to the course Modern History of
Israel, an independent state since 1948. In order to understand that history,
we need to begin with the emergence of the national movement of
the Jewish people known as Zionism. Jews existed in history as
a distinct community of faith for centuries, since antiquity. But the Zionist movement, as a Jewish
national movement, emerged only in modern age in the last third of the 19th century,
primarily, in central and Eastern Europe. Most historians explain the rise of
the Zionist movement as a reaction to the Jewish predicament. External economic difficulties, social
fragmentation, political persecution, as well as, internal decline of
religious form of Jewish life, caused many Jews to look for alternatives. They pursued various option to alleviate
their status, such as immigration to the new world, assimilation to
the gentile surrounding, finding refuge in the religious enclave, or
joining revolutionary socialist movement. The national option was one out of many,
and Zionism was a particular
form of Jewish Nationalism. When Zionism emerged in the late
19th century, the Jewish population in the world was about 8 million,
90% of them lived in the Europe, 5 million in eastern Europe, and
2 million in Central and Western Europe. About a million live in Asia,
North Africa, and North America combined. The hub of Jewish life was the area
in Eastern Europe known as the Pale of Settlement. This area belongs today to Poland,
Western Russia, Lithuania, Belarus, Ukraine, Galicia,
part of Hungary, and Romania. At the time of its emergence, only an insignificant fraction of the Jews
embraced the national values alternative. Today, Israel, which is the historical
outcome of the Zionist movement, holds the largest Jewish
population in the world.
