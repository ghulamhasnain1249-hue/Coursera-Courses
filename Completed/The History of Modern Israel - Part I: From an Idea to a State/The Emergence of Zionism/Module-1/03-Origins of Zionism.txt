What are the origins of
the Zionist movement? The Zionist Jews were influenced by
the phenomena of European nationalism. National movements, spread all over
Europe during the 19th century, where large groups of people
defined a collective identity by investing meaning to
ethnic historical data. They valued the collective origin,
ethnic particularities, unique language, past legends,
and traditional myth and symbols, and attempted to achieve
self-determination as a national state. A renaissance of a glorious past,
real or imaginary, become a central element
of the national movements. A quest for a future rebirth as a sovereign state
constitutes the major aspirations. Certain Jews adopted this attitude, and
tried to apply it to the Jewish people. They valued the ancient Jewish past of
antiquity as a glorious age of nation and sovereignty. Mourn their present agony and
grow in exile. And longed for a future revival as a renewed nation that will actively
return to it's ancestral land. Active return from exile to sovereignty constituted the essence
of the Zionist narrative. Such a goal can be envisioned
only by those who accept a modern world view of the enlightenment. That considered the human being
as an active agent of history. Among Jews it meant breaking with
the Jewish tradition of redemption. Instead of waiting passively for
a divine miracle and Messianic advent, the Zionists adopted active action to
build the revived nation of the future. In that sense, the Zionist movement
was a modern human endeavor, based on human responsibility. The persistent and even absurd of
anti-Semitism in modern Europe is another major contributor to
the emergence of the Zionist movement. Religious anti-Jewish hatred that
viewed Jews as heretic sinners in Christianity and as inferior unbelievers
in Islam gradually subside in modern time. But in many places, it was replaced
with a multi-dimensional anti-Semitism. The Jews were perceived as capitalist
exploiters as well as anarchists and socialist fanatics. Corrupted regime,
he framed the Jews as traitors and libeled them as political scapegoats. A new form of scientific anti-Semitism
known as racism emerged in the late 19th century in various places. Above all, the Jews were seen as
the ultimate other, and thus perceived by various segments of the general society
as a threat to its very existence. More visible in eastern Europe,
anti-Semitism reveal its ugly face through violent programs in Kishinev and
other towns. Prestigious institutions discriminated and
excluded Jews and right wing political parties
had an anti-Jewish platform. Anti-Semitic circles spread
the forged document known as the Protocols of the Elders of Zion. And disseminated many racists theories and
stereotypes. One of the most important
contributor to Zionism derived from disillusionment
from emancipation. In many places, the formal commitment
to grant equal rights to Jews was never implemented and
remained only in theory. Even in places where the emancipation
was applied, it challenged and even caused difficulties for those Jews who still adhered to a certain
tradition and a particular identity. Should they attend an important meeting
that is scheduled on their Jewish holiday? Should they take an exam that
is scheduled on Yom Kippur? Should they enlist in the army of
the country of their citizenship, without identifying with its
national characteristics? Only in a sovereign place of their own, they could probably solve
these identity problems. Many Jewish movement that emerged at
the end of the 19th century were aware of the Jewish discontent
in the modern world. Unhappy neither was complete
assimilation nor with religious isolation. This movement attempted to find
a specific solution to the Jews. Some advocated the creation of
a particular Jewish territory. Some envisioned an autonomous Jewish
community within a certain federation. And others promoted
the formation of a Jewish state government within
the post-revolutionary socialist world. In contrast to these options, the Zionists stressed their
affinity to the land of Israel. They advocated a solution outside of
Europe and endeavored to bring back the Jew to what they perceived was
the ancestral origin of the Jewish people.
