The Zionist movement was
never homogeneous, and reflected by birth orientation, social
context, and political affiliations. Practical Zionism seems to be
the earliest form of the movement. In Eastern Europe,
following pogroms against the Jews, many small organizations emerged that were
collectively called the Lovers of Zion. They urged their member to leave
Europe and to immigrate and settle in the land of Israel, which was
under the control of the Ottoman Empire. They supplied the demographic impetus for
what is known as the first and second wave of Zionist
immigration to Palestine. From the early 1880s up to
the break of the first world war. These groups advocated a practical
solution to the Jewish pogrom. Namely, to provide a Jewish life
by economic productivity, by building social institutions, and creating
a Hebrew culture in the new location. They called themselves pioneers. That meant revolutionizing their
life by embracing physical work, self organization,
new attachment to the land, and positive attitude towards manual and
agricultural labor. They aimed to build a new Hebrew
society based on self-determination. Basically ignoring the Arab
inhabitants of the land, they hoped to become a majority
in the new territory. They believed that
the creation of a modern, sovereign society would also
benefit the non Jewish population. These ideas influenced a few thousand
Jews primarily from small towns of eastern Europe who committed
themselves to building a new society in the land of Israel. They did not think in political terms
of establishing a state and rather stress the mission to rebuild a viable
Hebrew society and culture in Israel. One of the spokespersons of practical
Zionism was Yehuda Leon Pinsker, a physician who wrote the book,
Autoemancipation. In this book, he characterized the Jewish existence
in the diaspora as morbid and gloomy. Jews were despised and inferior, sick and degenerate, hated and
without self-dignity. For him, a Jewish productive revival
on the soil of the ancestral land of the Jews is a solution
to such a deplorable existence. The goal of practical Zionism was
thus a collective revival of the Jews from the abnormal existence in
the diaspora, an awareness that Jews, like every other nation on Earth need
their own place of actual development in order to become a normal and
self sufficient society. Practical Zionists strove for
immediate implementation of the ideas, and argue that the Jewish people should
not await until the nation of recognition. And start settling immediately,
in the land of Israel.
