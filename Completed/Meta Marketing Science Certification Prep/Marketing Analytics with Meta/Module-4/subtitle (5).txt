You now know what A/B tests are, when you can use them, how to create them on Facebook, and you can interpret
your results. I would like to
conclude this lesson by telling you a little
bit more about A/B tests on Facebook and the methodology that
is used to run them. A/B tests are a
form of experiment. So far we've seen two other
types of experiments you can run on Facebook: conversion lift tests and brand lift tests. We saw that conversion
and brand lift tests can be used to evaluate the effectiveness of
your advertising. A/B tests however are used for quick tactical decisions regarding your
advertising strategy. They're also quite
a bit cheaper to execute as you don't need to set aside as large of a budget as is required for
the lift tests. Both conversion lift and brand lift tests use a control group. People are randomly assigned to either your test
or control group, and after the test
you can evaluate the difference in results
between both groups. A/B tests do not use
a control group. In other words, there is no group that does not
get to see your ad. Instead, people are
randomly assigned to two different groups:
the A and the B group. The A group sees a
different version of your ads than the B group
and the difference in results between group A and B leads us to conclude
which ad is the winner. But it's important to realize
that people are assigned to the A and the B
group in random way, and once assigned to one of
these groups they will only see the version of the ad
associated with their group. Just as with the other
experiments on Facebook, an A/B test is carefully
planned ahead of time. You have to set it up before your campaign
starts running. Now you might wonder why would
you set up an A/B test and why you wouldn't just set up different campaigns
in ads manager. See how they perform and
then manually switch off the campaign that is
performing the worst. Switching campaigns of
manually in Facebook before a campaign
has ended may lead to inefficient delivery
and unreliable results as it won't let the Facebook optimization algorithms
run their course, hence you may end a campaign that might have
performed better after all. Also A/B testing helps
ensure your audience will be evenly split and
statistically comparable, while in formal testing can lead to overlapping audiences. Let's run to a quick
checklist that can help you prepare for an
A/B test on Facebook. Facebook advise us to
run the A/B test for 1-2 conversion cycles
or at least two weeks. Before deciding on a winner, makes sure the test has ended. You should also make
sure that the budget for both campaigns you test
is exactly the same, and it should be high enough so that tests
can be conclusive. Facebook advise us a minimum
weekly ad set budget that's equal to 50 times the
one-day cost per action, and a conservative minimum
weekly ad set budget must be equal to 100
times the average CPA. When it comes to the
creative you're testing, it's good practice
to always include prominent branding and
a clear call-to-action. That will give you most results and that will make
for a better test. As with other
experiments on Facebook, you should minimize
the overlap of the audience included
in your test with audiences for other
campaigns you maybe running as that may
contaminate your results. For similar reasons, you should also consider
a dark period pre and post your A/B test where
you don't run any ads. That way, results
from either your A or B group won't be influenced
by other campaigns. Keep those best
practices in mind and you can run a
successful A/B test. I encourage you to
use this test it really helps to improve
your ad campaigns.
