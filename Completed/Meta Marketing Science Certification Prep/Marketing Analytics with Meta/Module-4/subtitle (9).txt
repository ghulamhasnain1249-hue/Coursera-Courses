Marketing mix modeling has long
been used by marketers and analysts to understand how
marketing tactics impact sales. And it has proven to be an effective
method to produce accurate insights into the contribution of traditional
media channels like TV, radio, etc. However, the traditional marketing mix
modeling approach poses some challenges for today's complex, fast moving and
ever changing digital marketing channels. That's why when you work with digital
channels like Facebook for instance, there are a few things you should consider
to make your marketing mix model give you accurate results. Let's take a look at what that means. Platforms like Facebook offer a very
dynamic environment for your advertising. They allow you to adapt and optimize
your ads in real time, and they use sophisticated machine learning to improve
the results of your ads while they run. As you've probably gathered already,
Facebook's platform also evolves constantly, allowing advertisers to adapt
fast to ever changing consumer behavior. All this makes it harder to accurately
represent the data from a platform like Facebook in a marketing mixed model. However, it's not impossible. Facebook scientists and
partners have four suggestions for analysts who are integrating
Facebook data in their model. Let's look at them. The first recommendation is to
shorten the time frame used to evaluate the effect of your advertising. As we saw, traditionally marketing
mix modeling has required data from observations made over
the span of a year or more. But given the fast pace of innovation
on platforms like Facebook, waiting this long doesn't work as well for
digital channels. What's happening on these channels and the way consumers behave in
relation to them changes fast. So Facebook today is already quite
different from Facebook a year ago and it's definitely different
from two years ago or longer. While TV and radio maybe more stable,
digital channels are different. Of course, if you use a smaller time
interval, you will use less data and therefore the statistical power
of your model will suffer. Scientists have found ways to overcome
that door by analyzing several shorter timeframes for instance. So, they shouldn't stop you. And when you work with a partner, you should discuss
the timeframe they plan to use. Make sure it isn't too long and ask them
what they can do to increase the power of your test while still working
with shorter time frames. A second consideration when
including digital channels in your marketing mixed model is to make
sure to include the right data. With digital platforms, you get access
to much more granular insights and a lot more data than you can
get from traditional channels. It may be tempting to use all the data but
that's not a great idea. It's key to utilize the right inputs. Those inputs that can function
as independent variables that are comparable to the variables
you use for other channels. For instance, in the social media
marketing platform like Facebook, you get data about a number of likes and
comments. But the nature of that data is quite
different from what you get in traditional channels. So it won't be very useful to include
that in your marketing mix model. You may also be tempted to
include data about posts that went viral with metrics such
as the number of shares. But given that you run marketing mix
models to help you make decisions about future actions, these are not great
data points to include either. It's hard to make a post go viral so it isn't a strategy you can
easily repeat in the future. To make it easy for analysts to
include the right data for Facebook, Facebook partners can access the Facebook
marketing mix modeling portal, and directly get the data
that should be included. Having direct access to the staler
data makes it a lot easier to include the important insights from
campaigns on Facebook, Instagram and the audience network. When incorporating digital channels
into the marketing mix models, it's important to remember that not
all impressions are created equal. Or in other words,
not all campaigns are equally good. Therefore it's a good idea to include
some data to give some context around the quality of the campaign. When evaluating results
of marketing mix models. It's important to put some context around
the quality of the ads that were used as the quality of the creative used in
a channel will have an influence on the impact of that channel in the mix. It's also possible for analysts to
include quality scores in the marketing mix model to properly account for
the fact that ad quality will vary. Finally, there's one more thing that
can help to accurately account for the impact of digital channels
in marketing mix models. And that is using experiments to
calibrate your marketing mix model. When you run experiments, you get information on actual
lift caused by your ads. That information is very valuable data
to help tune the marketing mix model. It's a way for you to check the predictions of your
model against the actual ground truth. The information you get from your
experiment can then be used to update your model. Facebook has described its
