In the previous lesson, we focused on
conversion lift tests, one form of randomized
controlled trial experiments you can use to evaluate the
effectiveness of advertising. Conversion lift tests
focused on evaluating the effect of advertising
on conversions or actions. In this lesson we'll focus on a very similar
type of lift test, the Brand Lift Test. This test evaluates
the effectiveness of brands focused campaigns. Let's take a look. A brand Lift Test as
the name suggests, focuses on evaluating
brand lift, rather than conversion lift. Brand lift refers to an increase in the
awareness of a brand. The Brand Lift Test is an
experiment that helps you evaluate whether
your advertising has an effect on brand recall. A brand Lift Test works in exactly the same way a
conversion lift test works, but a brand lift test
is used for campaigns that have a goal related
to a brand outcome, for instance, increasing
brand awareness. Just as in a
conversion lift test you start by randomly assigning people from the
target audience of a campaign to either a
test, or control group. People in the test group
can be exposed to an ad, whereas people in the control
group don't see the ad. Since you're measuring
the effect of ads on brand perceptions
in a brand lift test, you'll measure your
results a little differently than you would
for a conversion lift test. For instance, you'll
want to check your ad's effect on brand awareness as
opposed to conversions, we measure that using a survey. All the people that were part of your test group
and the people in your control group
will be served one or a few polling questions. The main question asked is, do you recall seeing
an ad for this brand online or on a mobile device
in the last two days? This is how you measure
ad recall lift, which is an important metric in evaluating a brand
campaign success. But your specific ad recall lift question will of course, refer to the specific brand
that you're testing for. Next, you'll measure
the difference between the ad recall
in the test group, versus the control group. The effect you're seeing on brand recall is
caused by the ad. Let's look at an example
of how this test can help. Let's go back to Calla & Ivy, the flower business
in Amsterdam. Imre would like to sell more of her flower
bouquets online, but she also realizes
that not too many people know about the option to order flowers
through her website. She decides to run a campaign to generate more awareness of her brands and the fact that you can order online
from her website. She uses ads with beautiful images of the bouquets and headline that reads, "Treat yourself, order
our bouquets online," and she adds a learn more
call to action button. Imre wants to understand what the effect is
of this campaign. Before running the campaign
she decides that she will conduct a brand lift tests
to evaluate the effect. Out of Imre's target
audience a group of people is randomly assigned
to a control group, this group won't see Imre's ad. After the campaign runs
for about one month, a survey shown to people in Imre's test group who did have an opportunity to see the ad and her control group
people who didn't. Imre chooses to ask the
following two questions, "Do you recall seeing
an ad for Calla & Ivy online or on a mobile device
in the last two days? How familiar are you
with Calla & Ivy?" After the test is
complete Imre learns that her campaign was responsible for a 12 point lift in ad recall, and a 7 point lift
in familiarity. Imre is excited
about those results. Now you know what
brand lift tests are and why you might use them. Next, let's discuss how
