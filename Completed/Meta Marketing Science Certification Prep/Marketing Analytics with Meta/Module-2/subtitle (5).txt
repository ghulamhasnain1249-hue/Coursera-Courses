As you've learned

campaign objectives. Depending on the objective
you set for your campaign, Facebook will let
you select between different ad delivery
optimization options at the ad set level as
we've seen earlier. The objective and optimization choice you make while
you're setting up your campaign will directly influence who will see your ads. You are basically telling
Facebook to get you as much of the result you choose as
efficiently as possible. Your campaign objective and
optimization choices will also directly affect the
report you see in Ads Manager. That makes sense, of course, as you want to evaluate
the success of your campaign against what
you were trying to achieve. As we've seen in the last video, the key metrics to look
at when evaluating your campaign's success are
results and cost per result. Results are the number of times that your ad achieved an outcome based on the objective
and delivery optimization settings
that you selected. Cost per result
indicates how cost efficiently you
achieve the objectives that you set for
your ad campaign. It is the ratio of total amount spent to
number of results. In this video, we'll walk you through some of the most common combinations
of campaign objective and ad delivery
optimization so you get a better idea of what results
you would be measuring. Let's begin with the brand
awareness objective. The brand awareness objective is designed for advertisers who want to find people who are more likely to remember their ads. The ad delivery of brand
awareness campaigns is always optimized for the
so-called ad recall lift. In other words, your ads are shown to the
people Facebook estimates would remember seeing your ad if they were asked
within two days. If you run a brand
awareness campaign, the key metric you
would look at is the estimated ad recall lift, which is the estimated
number of people likely to remember your ads
within two days. To evaluate how efficient
your campaign was, you would look at the cost
per estimated ad recall lift, which is the average cost for each estimated ad recall lift. If you run multiple ad
sets and ads in parallel, you can, for example, evaluate which audiences
are more likely to remember your ads or which creatives
tend to be more memorable. Next, let's discuss
the traffic objective. This lets you drive people from Facebook or Instagram to any URL that you choose like your website's landing
page or a blog post. For traffic campaigns, you have a number of options
to optimize ad delivery. The two most common ones are link clicks and
landing page views. If you choose link clicks, then your ad will be shown to the people most likely
to click on them. If you choose
landing page views, then your ad will be shown
to the people most likely to click on it and load
the landing page. The page view information is provided by the
Facebook pixel so it needs to be embedded
in a landing page in order for you to
use the optimization. We talked about pixels in the first course
in this program, but I added a reading with
more information about the Facebook pixel and how
it works after this video. If you run a traffic campaign, the key metric you
would look at is either the number of link clicks or the number of
landing page views. To evaluate how efficient
your campaign was and which ad set or ad was
best at driving results, you would analyze the cost per click or the cost per
landing page view. This is the amount spent
divided by the number of link clicks or landing page
views your ad has received. Next, we have the
engagement objective. You select as objective
when you want to reach people who are more likely
to engage with your post, page, or event invitation. There are three
different types of engagement campaigns
in Ads Manager, each with their own ad
delivery optimization and respective key metric. First, if you want to drive engagement on a specific
post you're promoting, then you would optimize ad
delivery for post engagement. The key metrics you
would look at to evaluate your
campaign success are the number of post
engagements you have received and the cost
per post engagement. All reactions, comments, shares, and clicks on your post are
counted as post engagements. Second, if you want to
increase the number of likes your Facebook
business page has, you would optimize ad
delivery for page likes. The key metrics you would
analyze when evaluating campaign success
are the number of page likes and the
cost per page like. This counts both likes that happened directly on
the page after seeing your ad and likes
that happened on the page likes
button in your ad. Third, if you're
promoting an event and would like to increase
the number of responses, you would optimize ad
delivery for event responses. An event response is defined
as the number of people who responded interested or going
to your Facebook event. The key metrics you would look
at here are the number of event responses you have received and the cost
per event response. The final objective we will
briefly cover is conversions. In a conversion-focused
campaign, the goal is to reach
people who are more likely to take a certain
action you define or, in other words,
people who convert. The campaign we evaluated in an earlier video was a
conversion campaign. In that example, Inu and
Neko wanted to drive catalog requests on a specific landing
page they had set up. To evaluate campaign success, we looked at a number of catalog requests and the
cost per catalog request. However, business goals for
conversion campaigns can vary a lot based on what
pixel event you optimize for. This can range from
adding items to a basket, registering through a form, or making a purchase. The action you would
like people to take on your website after
seeing your ad is called an event that you define in Ads Manager and that is
tracked by the Facebook pixel. When it comes to evaluating your conversion
campaign success, you would look at a number of conversion events
you have received. In Inu and Neko's case, catalog requests and he would analyze the cost
per conversion event. There are more campaign
objectives and options for ad delivery
optimization to choose from than what we've
