In the previous video, I walked you through
the main components of the Ads Manager reports. Depending on your
campaign goals, you may find different
metrics in your report. We'll talk more about
that in our next lesson, but you may have reasons
to structure your report differently from the default reports you see in Ads Manager. For instance, you may want
to filter the data so you can focus on the metrics
that matter most to you, or you may want to
add a metric because it's an important KPI
for your organization. Fortunately, Ads Manager comes with a few useful
control features. Let's look at them
in this video. First, you have the search and filter option at the top of the
Campaigns page. This makes it easy to find any campaign ad set or ad you're looking for
by its name or ID. You just click on what
you want to search, such as campaign name, and then enter your search term. You can also use
the filter feature. Ads Manager comes
with a long list of predefined filters
you can pick from. Each of the filtered
categories you see here has subcategories, such as objectives, conversions, or audience age, 35-44. You can add multiple filters to help you uncover
specific insights. Third, you can change the
columns shown to you. To do this, go to the Columns button on the
right side of the page. The default columns report
is the performance report. You can also pick from other
predefined sets of columns. If you pick Engagement,
for example, you see all the
metrics that describe how often your posts were liked, saved, or shared. You can also customize
the columns you see by clicking on
"Customize Columns". One final control feature we'll want to check
out is the breakdown. You can find this
button to the right of the Columns 1 on the
right side of the page. Its drop-down menu
options allow you to break down the performance
of your campaign by time, who it was delivered to, and what actions people
took on your ads. When you pick Breakdown
by Week, for example, Ads Manager will break down all data by each week that
your ads were active. Each row here shows you the
data for a specific week. This can be a handy
tool to compare performance across different
time periods or audiences.
