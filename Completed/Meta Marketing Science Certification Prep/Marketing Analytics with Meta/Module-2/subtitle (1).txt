The beauty of digital marketing
is that you can start measuring results as soon as
a campaign starts running. Of course you won't get a
complete picture immediately, but you can get a
lot of information early on that will
help you evaluate how your campaign is
doing and make it possible to make some
corrections if necessary. In this lesson we'll have a closer look at
and learn how to understand some of your
campaign's performance metrics and results in Ads Manager. To do this we'll start at the campaigns dashboards
in Ads Manager. By default you'll see
a number of columns in Ads Managers that
run from left to right. We're going to go through and explain the most important ones. The information you
find in the report falls into three categories. First, their status and
campaign information. Second, you'll find results metrics and
finally cost metrics. Let's go through
them all starting with the status and
campaign information. In the first two columns you'll find the
campaign name and delivery column giving
you information about whether your campaign is
currently active or not. Next, you'll see the
bid strategy column. This shows the bid strategy you've selected
for each campaign. As a reminder, a bid strategy describes the way you
want Facebook to bid in the ad auction based on your cost goals and the
optimization events you choose. In this case we're running a lowest cost campaign so
that's what's indicated here. The next column displays
your campaign budget this is the maximum amount
you're willing to spend on your ad
sets or campaigns. It covers the average you're
willing to spend each day or what you're willing
to spend over your campaign or
ad sets lifetime. Next, we'll get into the actual performance metrics we'll start with
results metrics. The results column
measures the number of outcomes you've achieved
with your campaign. The metric you see
in this column is based on the ad objective or optimization you've
selected when you created your campaign. For example if you selected conversions as your
campaign objective, the results metric may
show the number of purchases that happened as a result of someone
seeing your ad. In our case we optimized
for traffic so the metric we see here is
the number of link clicks. In the next column we see the reach a campaign
has achieved. That's the number of people who saw your ads at least once. It's important to remember that reach is different
from impressions. Impressions is the metric we see in the next column and it indicates the number of times your ad was shown on screen. This does include the ads being displayed multiple times
to the same people, the reach measures the number of people that saw
your ad because of that an ads which can never be bigger than its number
of impressions. We can even do some
quick math here. If our reach was 10,000 and our campaign received
20,000 impressions then each person who one
of your ads were shown to on average
was shown it twice. Of course you will
always want to evaluate your results
against the costs, so that's where the
cost metrics come in. First we see the
cost per result. This is the average amount
it costs to achieve your key results like getting someone to click a
link or buy a product. Ads manager calculates this by dividing the number
in the amount spent column which we can see on the right by the number
in the results column, for example if you spend a $100 for a conversion ad and you received 50 conversions as a result your cost per
result would be $2. That's what would
appear in this column. The amount spent
column then gives you the estimated total
amount of money you've spent on your
campaign ad set or ad during its schedule. If your campaign is
currently running this metric maybe an estimate since it can take up to 48 hours for metrics to be processed. Next we have the ends column. This is where you'll find date your campaign is scheduled
to stop running. A campaign's end
date is based on the schedule you chose
in the campaigns ad set. If you didn't set an end date this column will read ongoing. All of the metrics we just
discussed are displayed for every campaign that was active
during a given date range. In our case that's
the last 30 days. With the date range filter, you can set the time period for which you'd like
to view metrics. Click on the button in
the top right corner which shows the current
date range to change it. You can set it to
dates that range from today to your campaign's
entire lifetime. Now let's take a look at the bottom row of
the campaign tab. Every campaign is shown
in a separate row and the last row will sum up
all of the metrics for you. You can see how much
money you've spent and how many people you've
reached for the time periods. Results will only be added up if all your campaigns were
optimized for the same result. As you can see from this
walkthrough Ads Manager gives you detailed reports on the performance
of your campaigns. Sometimes the volume
of data can be a bit overwhelming
and you may want to adjust the reports
a bit to only show the metrics that matter
most for your analysis. In our next video we'll
look at different filters Ads Manager lets you apply
to customize your report.
