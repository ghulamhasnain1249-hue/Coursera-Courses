the Facebook auction works. The Facebook ad auction is how Facebook
decides how much to charge for an ad. The Facebook ad auction is designed to
achieve two goals, creating value for advertisers by helping them reach and get results from people in their target
audience while also providing positive, relevant experiences for
people who use the Facebook products. To achieve this balance, they hold an auction to match the right
add to the right person at the right time. Each time there's an opportunity
to show an ad to someone, an auction takes place to determine
which ads to show to that person. Billions of auctions take place every
day across the Facebook family of apps. So who competes in these auctions? When an advertiser like you creates an ad, you define your target
audience on Facebook. But what happens when another advertiser
wants to target the same audience as you. Then both you and the other advertisers compete in
the auction to show that person your ad. For example say one advertiser
targets all women who like flowers and other advertiser targets all flower
lovers who live in California. In that case the same woman in California
who likes flowers could fall into both of these advertisers audiences. Let's call this woman Clara. So how does Facebook decide who's
add Clara sees, Facebook decides which add Clara sees by running an
equation that calculates a total value for each ad which is effectively
a numerical score. The ad with a higher total value,
wins the auction and gets shown to Clara. So what does this equation look like? An adds total value is
based on three things, the advertiser bid, the estimated
action rate and the ad quality. We'll explain each of them briefly. The advertiser bid is how much
an advertiser is willing to pay to achieve their desired outcome. Like a website visit or
a newsletter, sign up. You can manually set that bid or you can let Facebook automatically
determine what your bid should be. The more money you bid the higher
your advertiser bid will be. It's good to remember that the winner
of an auction is not simply the advertiser that pays the most. It's a combination of all
the factors in the equation. We'll talk more about setting bids later. The next part of the total value
equation is the estimated action rate. The estimated action rate measures
the likelihood that certain ad shown to a certain person will lead
to a desired outcome. It's basically an estimation of how
likely the user is to fulfill your ad campaign objective. Whether that's pressing a shop now
button or going to your website. The final part of the total
value equation is ad quality. Ad quality is determined by many sources, including feedback from people that view
or hide your ad as well as findings of low quality attributes such as sensational
language, engagement bait and more. So the advertiser bid,
the estimated action rate and the ad quality combined make up and
ads total value. So what does this mean for Clara who is
being targeted by two flower companies? Let's assume both companies
ran an ad with the goal of getting people to purchase
flowers from their online website. They input the same advertiser bid and Facebook determined Clara is equally
likely to fulfill their desired goal? Or in this case byproducts
from both of them. This means the estimated action
rate is the same for both ads too. Advertiser A however, is sending people
to a website that has a lot of ads on their homepage as well as misleading
information about flower pricing. That's not a great user experience. Hypothetically this could
lower advertiser As ad quality score driving total value down. This means advertiser B, would have
a higher total value score leading to them winning the auction and
Clara seeing their ad. So now that you understand the auction, how does that relate to how
much you are charged for an ad. Billions of auctions take place every day
across the Facebook family of apps and often with far more than two companies. Every time you see an ad is
the result of an auction. And Facebook only charges you for
those ads when you win the auction. Facebook charges you by
the number of times your ad is displayed to people also
known as an impression. Facebook charges on 1000 impression basis
and that's known as CPM or cost per 1000. CPM measures the total amount
spent on an ad campaign divided by impressions multiplied by 1000. So an example is if you spend
50 dollars on your ad and got 10,000 impressions,
your CPM would be $5. You might be wondering
why CPM is important. CPM is a common metric that's used by
the online advertising industry to gauge the cost effectiveness of an ad campaign. It's often used to compare performance
among different ad publishers and campaigns. So while you get charged $50 you
can gauge what you were charged for by the CPM price. For some marketing goals you can also
choose to be charged per link click or by action. This means Facebook will only charge you
if customers click on your ad link or take another specific actions
such as watching a video. We will show you later where you can make
the choice to be charged for link click or action rate rather than impression. Now that you know how the auction works
and how Facebook charges for ads. You are ready to set your budget in ads

$100 to run an Ad over a month but Facebook only spends $70 that month

payments with manual payments. You preload money to your
