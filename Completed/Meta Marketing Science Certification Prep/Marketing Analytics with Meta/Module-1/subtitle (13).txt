Before we look at how to create audiences in Facebook Ads
Manager, here's a story. Your niece's birthday is coming up and you want to
get her a gift. She's a teen who loves to read, so you head to the bookstore, where you ask a
bookseller for some help. Instead of pointing the way to the young adult fiction section and leaving you to browse, the bookseller asks
you some questions, "How old is she?
Where does she live? What does she like to
do in her spare time? What other books or
movies did she enjoy?" You answer, "She's 16. She lives outside
of New York City. She loves to draw, and is into stuff like
The Hunger Games." Based on that information, the bookseller pulls a
book from the shelf about a teenage girl who
saves the world in a dystopic New York City, and the book is full
of cool illustrations. You give your niece the book
and she loves it saying, "This is the perfect gift." How does this relate to
Facebook Ads Manager? Well, because your teenage niece was the target audience
for that book, if you think about
your business, you're creating a
product that you want to get into the
hands of people who are going to say that it is the perfect gift or exactly
what they were looking for, instead of advertising
to everyone on the planet and wasting
your dollars doing so. You want to focus on
those who will be most interested in
you and your product, and who will be most
likely to buy it. Creating a target
audience isn't so much about targeting certain
people by name, but choosing characteristics
of groups of people that line up with who you think would be interested
in your business. For your niece, it was a teenage girl who
likes to read, likes books, liked
The Hunger Games, lives in New York City, and loves to draw. When you advertise on Facebook, you can specify the audience you would like to
show your ads to. You can do that by creating
an audience for your ads. On Facebook, there are three different ways
to create an audience. You can define a core audience, a custom audience, or a lookalike audience. Each audience type can fulfill your advertising
objectives in different ways so you can utilize the right audience
for each campaign you run. Let's go through an overview
of the three types. You'll create and
define audiences in Ads Manager at the Ad Set level. While you can assign different audiences
to each campaign, you can't further segment
audiences per ad, which means whatever
audience you choose will apply to all ads in your ad set. The first type of
audience you can create is your core audience, which is an audience you create manually by choosing all
the characteristics of the people you want to see
your advertising because you believe they'll be the most interested and the
most likely to act. Here's a quick overview
of how you set up a core audience
in Ads Manager. The audience screen will
default to create new audience, and you can create your
core audience here. First, pick the location
where you want to advertise. You can target whole countries, cities, or even
specific zip codes. Next, set the age
of your audience. If you know that your business
caters to millennials, for example, set the age
to just that generation. Set the gender targets of your audience either
men, women or all. Only set a language if you're targeting non-native
speakers in your location, otherwise, just leave it blank. The detailed targeting
section is where you'll be able to drill down into
specific demographics, such as education
level and occupation, interests such as
hobbies and pastimes, and behaviors such
as device usage. Finally, on the connections, you can choose to include or exclude people who
interact with your page, app, or events. In other words, you can
exclude people who are already following you to find new
audience members for instance. Your core audience is set. Use this audience to find the people who should know
about your products or services and either
start them on their customer journey or raise their awareness
of your brand. The second type of audience
is a custom audience, which allows you
to target people who have already interacted with your business in
some way and who you want to nurture along
their customer journey. For example, a custom audience could target long-time
customers with an ad or people who've expressed interest by visiting
your app or website. This targeting of customers or potential customers
who have had some touchpoint
with your business already is called retargeting. Let's have a look at
how you can create a custom audience
in Ads Manager. In the audience section, at the Ad Set level, we click on "Create New"
under Custom Audiences. Now, we can choose from two different data sources
to build our audience; our own data or data
from Facebook sources. Our own data sources allow
us to target people who visited or performed
certain actions on our website or
in our mobile app. This information is tracked
through the Facebook pixel, the SDK, or the conversion API. We talked about them in the
first course of this program. We can also build a
custom audience by uploading a list of online
or offline customers. With Facebook data sources, we can build a custom
audience targeting those who watched a video we posted
or filled out a lead form, visited our Instagram profile
or Facebook business page, or interacted with an
event we've created. You can then pair your
custom audience with criteria such as
location, age, gender, and other demographics,
interests, and behaviors to achieve an
even more targeted audience. The third audience
type you can create in Ads Manager is a
lookalike audience, which is an audience
that's built off of an existing custom audience and looks like your
current customers in terms of demographics, interests, and behaviors. The thinking is that because your current customers are
interested in your business, people like them will
probably be interested too. First, you'll start with
a source audience which will be a custom audience
like we just explored. Use data from your
website or app, upload an email list, or use the Facebook
data sources we've seen earlier to create
this source audience. Next, set a location
you want to target, for example, you could target similar audiences in
different regions. Using the information
you provide, Facebook will then
find and target people with similar
characteristics. Facebook will also
allow you to select how closely you want the audience to look like your
source audience, from very similar
to less similar. The broader you go, the bigger the audience will be. You can also create multiple lookalike
audiences with different similarity sizes, and use them for the same
or different ad sets. This was an overview of
the three audience types you can create in Ads
Manager: core audiences, custom audiences, and
lookalike audiences. By carefully specifying
the criteria of your ideal customers and using the information you've collected on your loyal customers, you have a better shot at
finding new customers. In other words,
what you get out of Ads Manager audiences is only
as good as what you put in.
