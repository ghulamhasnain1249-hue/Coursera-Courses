Now that we know how to
determine a campaign budget, let's talk about how we set
that budget in Ads Manager. First it's important to
remember that a budget you set an Ads Manager is not necessarily the
amount you'll spent. For example you don't buy
an ad campaign for $100. You instead indicates to
Facebook how much you want to spend on an ad campaign
over a given time period. If you set a maximum
budget of $100 to be spent over a
month but Facebook only spend $70 in advertising that month based on the
campaign's performance, your bill will be $70. When you first set up a
campaign in Ads Manager, you have the option
to either set a campaign budget or set
individual ad set budgets. Setting a budget at
the campaign level allows Facebook to
dynamically assign budgets to ad sets based on
their performance therefore maximizing your
spending efficiency. This tool is known as
campaign budget optimization. Setting a budget at the ad
set level lets you control how much money you spent
on each individual ad set. In general we recommend
setting your budget at a campaign level if you
have two or more ad sets. That's because if you
set up your budget at the ad set level you'll
end up paying more per desired outcome because
you won't benefit from Facebook's campaign budget
optimization option. As we mentioned before Facebook's campaign budget
optimization directs your advertising dollars
to the ad sets that are providing the best outcome
at any given moment in time. This means that if you have two active ad sets
in one campaign, Facebook might
spend 90 percent of your budget on the
first ad set if that asset is getting most efficient outcomes
based on your objective. Campaign budget
optimization uses machine learning to optimize
your campaign budget. It's almost impossible
for us humans to replicate that degree of
precision and calculation. While you may feel tempted to optimize your campaign
budget yourself, we recommend enabling campaign budget
optimization and letting the machine do the
work for you if you choose to use multiple ad sets. Once you click the Campaign
Budget Optimization toggle it'll prompt you to enter a budget for
your campaign. Facebook offers two types of budgets to control
how much you spent on a campaign or ad set: daily budgets and
lifetime budgets. We'll briefly go over each. When you set your daily budget, you're setting an
average amount you're willing to spend on a
campaign every day. The actual amount spend can
vary slightly day-to-day. Facebook may spend
up to 25 percent more than what you set as a daily budget if they see potential for better
results on a certain day. But if they spend
more on a day with lots of opportunities for
good campaign results, they'll spend fewer on a
day with less which means your daily budget will always average out to your set amount. When you set a lifetime budget you're telling Facebook how much you're willing
to spend over the entire run-time
of your campaign. You will never be charged more
than your lifetime budget. In most cases Facebook
will aim to spend your budget evenly over
your campaign's lifetime. Let's say we set a
lifetime budget of $700. We'll then provide Facebook with the schedule on
the ad set level. Once we've set up our
ad set schedules, Facebook will know our
full campaign runtime. They'll make sure to
spread out to budget more or less evenly
during that time. There are few more
options we can use to optimize our
campaign strategy. We'll cover them
