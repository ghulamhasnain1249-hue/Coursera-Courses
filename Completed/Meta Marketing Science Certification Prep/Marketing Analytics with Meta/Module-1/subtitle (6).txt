When marketers
create campaigns to advertise their
products or services, they usually create more than
one ad and in many cases, they will have specific messages for different audience segments. That's why tools to create advertising online are typically structured at different levels, letting advertisers
create one campaign that contains multiple ads. That's also the case for Facebook Ads Manager
and in order to understand and evaluate
the success of campaigns, it's important to
understand that structure. In this video, I'll explain it. While marketing
analysts may not be the ones creating the campaigns, knowing how it
works will be very helpful when you're asked
to evaluate the results. There are three parts to
a Facebook ad campaign. The campaign, an ad set, and the individual ads. Collectively, this is called
the campaign structure. Knowing how these parts work
together can help marketers best optimize their advertising
experience and results. We'll briefly break
down each part using one of our
example companies. Calla & Ivy, a flower
shop in Amsterdam with a website where people can
order bouquets online. We'll start by
explaining the campaign. The first thing you
do when you go to create an ad in Ads Manager is, choose a campaign objective. Think of the campaign as
the foundation of your ad. The objective you
choose will define what options you will have in the rest of the
campaign setup. The objective you choose should
reflect what you want to accomplish with your ads
such as promoting your app, or building awareness
for your business. We'll come back to this in more detail later but let's say Calla & Ivy want to drive
traffic to their website. They'll set, increase
web traffic as their campaign objective
and move on to the next level of the
structure, ad sets. Ad sets are at the second
level of a campaign. Think of the ad set as the level that tells your ad how to run. At the ad set level, you will define who will see
your ad or the targeting, where the ad will show up, the placement, how much you
want to spend the budget, and how long it should
run for the schedule. It's important to
know a campaign can include multiple ad sets, each with different targeting, scheduling, and budgeting
option selected. This allows you
to easily segment your ads to try different
things to see what works. You could target one ad
set to young people, and one ad set to older ones. You could set one ad
set to run on Facebook, and one to run on Instagram. You can effectively
use them to target any one of Facebook's
audience categories. For example, Calla
& Ivy might want to advertise wedding
flower packages to newly engaged couples in the Netherlands in March
and they're interested in finding out whether
younger or older couples respond better to their ads. They could create one ad set to target people who
are newly engaged, who live in the
Netherlands and are between the ages of 20 and 35, and a second ad set to target
those between 36 and 49. The third and final level of the campaign structure
is the ad level. The ad level defines what your audience
will actually see, for which you first
choose the ad format, and then you'll add
videos, or photos, right copy, and define
your destination. Keep in mind that you can have multiple ads within
a single ad set. Calla & Ivy could for
example test photo ads and a video ad in each ad set, to learn which performs better. To summarize, a
campaign is built off of a single
objective and consists of one or multiple ad sets that each contain one
or multiple ads. It's a simple structure
that helps you organize and optimize
your campaign. This structure also
enables you to test many different parameters such as your audience,
ad creative etc. This then allows you
to shift more budget towards the highly
performing ad set or ads, or consider new
strategies altogether. You now know how ads are bought and sold on
