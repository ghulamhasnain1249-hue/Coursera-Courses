One of the crucial skills of a data analyst is understanding trends
and patterns over time, such as fluctuations and stock prices or changes
in weather patterns. In this video, we will explore
how time series analysis helps us recognize
these trends and cycles and data collected
over time intervals, enabling us to make informed predictions and
strategic decisions. Let's start by defining what
time series analysis is. Time series analysis
examines how quantitative variables
change over time, often to forecast future values. Like the other models
you have learned, time series analysis describes a broad category of analyses, but they all share a few things. First, they all need to use time as one of
their variables. That said, time can be
written in terms of days, weeks, months, or any
other unit of time. As a best practice, you should gather data in
the smallest unit you can. Next, we will discuss the most common assumptions used for time series analysis. First, we need to talk about
the minimum amount of time. In time series analysis, it's assumed that
the data covers a sufficiently long period to capture underlying patterns, trends, and seasonal variations, ensuring the model accounts for typical fluctuations
and cyclical changes. The next assumption
is dependence, and to meet this assumption, all observations
should originate from the same source with new values recorded
sequentially over time. Some time series analyses
require stationarity, meaning the mean value should
remain constant over time. For example, if
financial data is analyzed over a period
affected by inflation, the increasing mean
could skew the analysis. If a plot of your data
trends upward or downward, indicating a changing mean, this assumption isn't met. However, there are methods
to adjust for this. Now, you should be able to describe the assumptions
for a time series, which you can apply using statistical software to discover trends or patterns over time. Get ready for the next steps
and start leveraging and analyzing real-world situations
using this analysis.
