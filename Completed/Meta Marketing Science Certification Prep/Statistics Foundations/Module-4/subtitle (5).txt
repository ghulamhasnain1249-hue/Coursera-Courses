One of the
foundational skills of a data analyst is the ability
to find the relationship between two things like exercise and mental health or study
hours and test scores. In this video, we
will examine how a simple linear regression
helps us to see the relationship
between two variables and use it to make
data driven decisions. Remember, a regression
analysis predicts a dependent variable given
an independent variable. When we refer to something
as regression analysis, this is an entire group of different analyses that
all predict relationships. In this course, we will focus on simple linear regression
which is when a line is used to predict the
relationship between one independent and
one dependent variable because there is
a steady rate of change between the
two variables. Now that we know what it is, let's review how it works. The first step is to
gather your data. You will need an
independent variable and a dependent variable. To review, an
independent variable is a variable in an experiment
or test you control. For example, in a plant
growth experiment, the amount of water
given to the plants is the independent
variable because you decide how much water
each plant receives. While a dependent
variable is the one that changes based on responses
to the independent variable. Using our plant example, the dependent variable
would be the growth of the plants measured in
terms of height or biomass, because it depends
on and changes in response to the amount
of water they receive. Next, you will plot your
data points on a graph. The independent variable
will be the x-axis, the horizontal line
at the bottom, and the dependent variable
will be the y-axis, the vertical line on the side. Once you plot your data, you draw a line sometimes
called the line of best fit. This is a straight
line that most accurately represents
the layout of the data. Note how the data is
clustering around the line. Now that you have
your data plotted out and a line that
describes your data, you can use it to
make predictions. There are a few
different ways to make predictions based on
how you drew the line. If you created an
equation for the line, you can plug your
independent variable into it to calculate
your dependent variable. If a software application
draws the line, you can simply
look at the chart. You can then draw a
line from that point on the y-axis to find the
value on that side. In this graph, the value on the x-axis is followed to the
line on the red path. The green path then goes from that location on the
line to the y-axis. This way, it predicts a dependent variable from
an independent variable. Let's explore some
other common terms associated with a simple
linear regression. First, there is
the term residual, which is sometimes
called the error. A residual is the
difference between where the data point
is and where the line expects it to be or simply how far the data
point is from the line. In this image, every
data point has its own residual because each point is different
from the line. Another term used in simple linear regression
is what's called the R^2. R^2 sometimes called the
coefficient of determination, is how much of the variance in the dependent variable is explained by the
independent variable. In other words, is your independent variable good at predicting your
dependent variable? There are multiple
ways to calculate R^2. But many software programs will have the option to
calculate it for you. What are the good or
bad values for R^2? There isn't really
any value good or bad because R^2 doesn't have specific cutoff scores since it is used
to compare models. Last, let's go over the assumptions for
simple linear regression. The minimum sample size for
linear regression is 20. This means that you need
at least 20 data points or separate observations before you can run this analysis. The next assumption
is linearity. Linearity is the idea that
there is a relationship between the two variables and plotting them
will give you a line. Another assumption
is homogeneity of variance, sometimes
called homoscedasticity. Homogeneity is ensuring there is roughly the same
amount of variance in each variable being compared. Next is normality, which is the idea that your data
creates a normal distribution. Finally, we get to the last
assumption, independence. This is not an assumption
we have seen before, but it is not very complicated. Independence means
every observation is unique and stands alone. Now, you should be able to
describe the steps to complete a simple linear
regression which you can start applying using any
statistical software. Get ready for the next steps
and start leveraging and analyzing real world situations using this type of analysis.
