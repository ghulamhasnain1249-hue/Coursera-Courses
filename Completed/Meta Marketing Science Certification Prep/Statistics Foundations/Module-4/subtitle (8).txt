Although we won't cover specific classification
analyses in detail in this course, it's crucial to grasp
their importance. For example,
classification models are integral in
medical diagnostics, where they help
distinguish between benign and malignant tumors
based on patient data. Understanding these
models allows for the practical
application of data to predict categories and make informed decisions
in various fields. In this video, we will review the most common classification
models and their use. A classification model
uses one or more variables to predict a categorical or
discrete dependent variable. In other words, it is a lot
like a regression model, which uses an
independent variable to predict a dependent variable. The main difference is the
variable type they predict. While a regression model predicts a
quantitative variable, a classification model predicts
a qualitative variable. A few popular
classification models include K-nearest neighbors, random forest, logistic
regression or binary regression, Naive Bayes, or support
vector machines. Each of these analyses take
a very different approach, but all attempt to predict a categorical
dependent variable. There are many uses for
classification analysis. Let's explore a few. Classification models help
in diagnosing diseases by analyzing patient data and symptoms to predict
health conditions. In addition, financial
institutions use classification to assess the credit-worthiness
of individuals to determine things
like eligibility for credit cards and loans. Another use is when businesses
apply classification to group customers
based on purchasing behavior or preferences to
tailor marketing strategies. Last, these models
are used in analyzing public sentiment from
text and reviews, social media and more, classifying them as positive,
negative, or neutral. Now, we have talked about what
classification models do. We listed a few of the more popular analyses and went over how they are
used in data analytics. As you grow your skills
and data analysis, you'll begin to learn more about classification analysis and how you can use it to
your advantage.
