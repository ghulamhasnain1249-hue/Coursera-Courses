Have you ever seen potential
storm models and forecasts? These forecasts are a great
example of statistical modeling. Storm tracking systems use years of
weather data to predict a storm's path. In this video, we will define statistical
modeling and explore its use. Statistical modeling is the act of
taking a statistical analysis method and applying it to our data to create
a mathematical representation or a model. Essentially, statistical modeling
is the process of creating a simplified mathematical formula
based on real world data to predict or explain patterns and relationships. Lets continue to use our weather example. A meteorologist or meteorological
software would need to follow several steps to create a statistical
model to predict storms track. First, meteorologists gather years
of weather data such as storm paths, wind speeds, and atmospheric pressures. They then apply statistical
analysis methods to this data. For our storm prediction, they might
use regression analysis, which helps in understanding how the path of a storm
relates to various weather factors. They also may adopt a term
like regression model, since often the name of a model is
reflected by the analysis it uses. The analysis leads to the creation
of a mathematical model, essentially a formula that
summarizes these complex relationships that can
be represented visually. With this model, meteorologists input
current data like wind speed and direction into the formula
when a new storm forms. The model then predicts the storms future
path based on historical patterns. This image shows a hurricane as
it's approaching the east coast of the United States. Each line that curves along
the coast is a different predicted track based on various statistical and
computational models. These paths illustrate the possible
routes the storm might take, each informed by a unique
combination of data, assumptions, and analytical calculations. Statistical models aren't
just about predictions, they're about potential realities. Statistical models offer insights
that drive proactive measures and inform decision making.
