So far we have reviewed guidelines for choosing
the appropriate model. Now, it's time to show
what you know by applying different models to some
example case studies in the field of marketing. A local gym owner, Alex, is planning an expansion due to a rapid increase
in membership. He has collected data on
how long each member has been with the gym and their
frequency of facility usage. Alex aims to predict
gym usage based on membership duration to inform his expansion decision
using data-driven insights. Alex wants to know if a member has been a member
for over two years, how many times will they
visit the gym in one month? In this case, simple
linear regression is the best model
to figure out why. We first identify the variables that we need to use to give
us the result we need. The independent variable, or what we are controlling, is the duration of membership, which is a discrete or
quantitative variable. The dependent variable
or what we're measuring is the number of times the member visits the gym. Now that we know our variables, let's figure out the purpose. The purpose is to predict
the dependent variable, number of gym visits, based on a specific value of the independent variable or
duration of gym membership. This is based on
the historic data that Alex has
collected over time. Looking back at our definitions, this describes simple
linear regression. Let's look at another example. Carlos from Inu and Neko has recorded the number of
sales received from ads he's included in Inu
and Neko's monthly e-newsletter every
month for five years. He wants to know not only how sales from the e-newsletter
ads are doing, but how they will do next year to find out if they're
worth continuing. You already have the
current number of sales generated by
the ads in the past, so how many sales can
Carlos from Inu and Neko expect to receive from
e-newsletter ads next year? In this case, time series is
the most appropriate model. To determine why, let's first
identify our variables. Our independent
variable is time. These metrics are
recorded every month. The dependent variable
is the number of sales that come
from e-newsletter ads. This is a discrete or
quantitative variable. Now that we have our
variables identified, let's consider the purpose. The entire point is to
forecast the number of sales, so we will have an
approximate idea of what they will be next year. This describes time
series analysis. Now, some situations may be
more complicated than others. But if you take your time, identify the variables and ensure you understand
the purpose, you can figure out which
model you should use.
