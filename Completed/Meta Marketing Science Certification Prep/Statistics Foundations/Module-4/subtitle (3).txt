Statistical models are like putting
on glasses for the first time. Looking at data makes it difficult
to get a clear picture of trends, patterns, relationships, and predictions. When you view the data through
the lens of a model, the trends, patterns, relationships, and
predictions become crystal clear. In this video, we will explore some of
the most common statistical models, starting with the regression model. A regression model is a versatile
statistical model for measuring the relationship
between variables. A simple linear regression
model uses a straight line to describe the relationship
between two variables. It looks at one independent variable,
x, and one dependent variable, y, and tries to draw a straight line
that best fits the data points. This line is used to predict
the value of y based on x. Visually, it appears as a series
of data points on a graph with a straight line passing through them, where the line represents the predicted
relationship between x and y. How does a regression model work? Let's consider the following example. A real estate company wants to
understand how the size of a house using the measure of square
footage affects its selling price. They collect data on
various homes they've sold, plotting the size of each
house against its sale price. The shape of the scatter plot
is approximately a line. By applying a simple linear
regression model to this data, they can predict the sale price
based on the house's size, with the model providing
a straight line that best represents the relationship
between the two variables. Another common type of statistical
model is a classification model. A classification model is
a type of statistical model that predicts categorical variables. A classification model
categorizes data points into predefined groups or
classes based on their attributes. It examines multiple variables
to assign each data data point to a specific category. Visually, it groups data points based on
their characteristics and relationships. This could be in graph form or a chart. For instance, in this image,
blue circles are used for class a and
green triangles are used for class b. On the chart, a straight line or
decision boundary divides the graph into two regions
separating the two classes. The model determines
the placement of the line. Points on one side of the line
are predicted to belong to class a and points on the other to class b. What does this classification
look like in the real world? Let's consider an example
in the finance world. A bank wants to predict which
loan applicants might default. It uses a classification model that
looks at past applicants data, like credit scores and repayment history, to classify applicants as high risk or
low risk for defaulting on their loans. Next, let's talk about a cluster model. A cluster model is a type of statistical
model that identifies groups or segments of an audience. A cluster model organizes data
into groups where the members of each cluster share certain traits,
effectively segmenting the overall population into smaller,
similar subsets. Visually, it looks like a scatterplot, where each cluster of data
points is separated from others, often circled or colored differently
to distinguish between the groups. This grouping helps identify patterns or
segments within the data, such as customer types or
product categories. But how does the cluster model work? Let's consider the following example. A retail company collects
customer purchase data and wants to tailor marketing strategies
to different segments of customers. Using a cluster model,
they group customers into clusters based on purchasing patterns like frequency,
average spending, and product preferences. Each cluster represents a customer
segment with similar behaviors, allowing the company to
customize marketing efforts for each group effectively. The last model that we will
discuss is a time series model. A time series model is
a statistical model that predicts a variable based on what
it has done in the past. A time series model predicts future data
points based on historical trends and patterns. Visually, it looks like a line graph
with time on the x axis and the variable of interest, such as stock prices or
temperature readings on the y axis. This model fits a line or
curve that moves through the data points to indicate how the variable
changes over time. But what types of situations can
a time series graph be used for? Let's consider you're a business owner and
you have a chain of coffee shops. Your managers record daily sales for
several years, and you see this information to
create a time series model that forecasts future sales patterns
based on historic ones. This model can help predict the expected
sales during different seasons or days of the week, allowing for
better inventory and staffing management. These four models are the most common
ones you will likely encounter in your role as a data analyst. Be sure to leverage the visuals that
a model gives you in order to get a clear picture of trends, patterns,
relationships, and predictions.
