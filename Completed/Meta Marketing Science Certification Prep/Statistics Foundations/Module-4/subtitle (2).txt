Data shapes our day from our morning
routine to our evening wind down. It helps us find patterns like
the link between coffee and work performance, or
steps taken in overall well-being. In the commercial world,
sales data sharpens business tactics and patient data refines care
approaches in healthcare. Statistical models are key tools
that convert this information into actionable insights, guiding decisions
in everyday life and critical sectors. In this video, we will explore
examples of common statistical models. First, let's review the definition
of linear regression. Simple linear regression
is a versatile model for measuring the relationship
between two variables. In data analytics,
it is usually used as a predictive model. For example, Carlos, at Enu and
Neko, has a record of how much they have spent on Facebook ads and how many
times the ad banners were clicked. A simple linear regression tells you
the past data to predict future outcomes. Using a simple linear regression,
Carlos can see that if he spends a certain amount of money on Facebook
ads next month, he can expect to get around a certain number of clicks
based on data he has from the past. In this way, he can determine
precisely how much he wants to spend on advertising to
achieve his desired results. Another common model used in data
analysis that we're going to explore is cluster analysis. A cluster analysis is a common
statistical model that groups similar items into clusters
based on shared characteristics. Essentially, it identifies patterns and data by organizing items with
similar characteristics together, which can reveal natural groupings and
trends, or classified data points into different
categories based on their attributes. Let's look at an example
of a cluster model. Amara, from Cala & Ivy, wants to
better understand her customer base so she can target her marketing efforts to
groups with specific interests or needs. However, plotting data on their
customer base might result in a scatter plot that doesn't tell
them much about their customers. Clustering will mathematically break
this plot down into groups or clusters. Amara can then see specific
subsets of her customer base so she knows how to target her
next advertising campaign. The last model we will explore
is a time series model. The time series model uses
a time series analysis, a statistical technique that
analyzes a sequence of data points, typically measured at successive points, to identify trends, cycles, and patterns. This analysis helps forecast future
values based on historical data. It is often used in economics,
weather forecasting, and investing. Lets revisit Enu and Neko for
an example of this model. Carlos, from Enu and Neko, has kept track of his sales every day for
the last three years. Time series analysis will
allow him to forecast or predict his sales for the next month. With these projections,
he can plan accordingly. These statistical models are going
to help you confidently approach data as you'll be able to use the right
analysis to give you the insights you need to make more informed
data-driven decisions.
