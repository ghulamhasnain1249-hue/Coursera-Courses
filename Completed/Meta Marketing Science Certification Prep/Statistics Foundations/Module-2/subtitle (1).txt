There are roughly 7.7 billion people on the
planet right now. The logistics and cost of understanding the
interests, behaviors, and purchasing preference of the world's population
are astronomical. Even if you could, the data would have changed by the time you finish
gathering it. Luckily, you don't
need information on every living human for
an accurate analysis. All you need is a sample. This video will explore
why samples are used in many fields to understand
large groups of people better. Sampling is a basic
statistical method for choosing a small, manageable group from
a larger population. This allows us to estimate
the characteristics of the whole population without the need to study every
one or every thing, saving time,
resources, and effort. All you need is a sample, a carefully selected subset of the population intended
to represent the whole. Let's use an example of
a marketing firm that wants to know why people
bought a certain car model. Surveying all drivers of a
specific car model and style is challenging due to the large number of
global and local drivers. To reach each car owner, you must find
contact and confirm that each driver owns
and uses that car model. This process involves
significant time, effort, and financial resources, and due to the dynamic nature
of car ownership and usage, a comprehensive dataset
is not guaranteed. This is why we use sampling. Sampling helps by choosing a smaller representative
group of drivers to study. This enables us to learn about the wider populations traits and preferences without having to survey a very large
group of drivers, making research
doable and efficient. As this example shows, sampling has several benefits. One benefit of sampling
is that it saves time. Surveying an entire
population is time consuming with inevitable non responses requiring follow up efforts. By carefully selecting a
representative sample, you can significantly save time and effort and
contacting participants. Another benefit of
sampling is that it saves money by reducing
research costs while still enabling
researchers to obtain the same insights from a smaller group as they would
from the entire population. Because you're gathering data
from fewer participants, you have an opportunity to ask more questions
of each of them, getting more points of
data per participant. Research aims vary. Sometimes they involve
gathering brief data from many like surveys on a receipt
while other times, they involve gathering
more detailed data from a few like focus groups. Sampling enables you to
ask more questions and gather richer data than
using an entire population. Using sampling, researchers
can efficiently gather precise data on specific
groups like car owners, despite the challenges of
collecting large scale data. In future videos, we
will discuss choosing the right sample size
and other factors to enhance sampling in
different research areas.
