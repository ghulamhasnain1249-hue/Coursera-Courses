Imagine a research study claiming that
100% of people prefer a single soda brand. That high of a value is shocking, right? Now, what would you think if you
found out the study's conclusion was based on a very small
sample size of only ten people? This startling statistic
suddenly seems less reliable, highlighting the importance
of sample size in research. So how many people should be in a sample? This might feel like an impossible
question, but we can use a few guiding principles in statistics to
answer exactly that question, starting with the central limit theorem. The central limit theorem is a statistical
premise that given a sufficiently large sample size from a population
with a finite level of variance, the mean of all sampled variables
from the same population will be approximately equal to
the mean of the whole population. Now that definition is a lot to digest,
lets break this definition down. The central limit theorem tells us
that with a large enough sample size, the samples average and variation will
closely match the whole populations. So while you dont have to
ask the whole population, getting a large enough sample
will give the same results. Which is why larger samples
give us more reliable insights. But how many is large enough? Various methods and online calculators
can help determine the right sample size. Its important to meet the minimum
sample size needed for accurate analysis, which we'll cover
in more detail later in this course when we examine specific
types of analysis. Another question to think about
is can a sample be too large? When a sample is too large, we see
something called the plateau effect. The plateau effect is a phenomenon where
after a certain point, additional effort, investment, or data no longer results
in significant improvement or progress. In the context of data analysis, it refers to the point at which
increasing the sample size further adds little to no value to the accuracy or
quality of the results. This means you're spending
resources gathering more data for little to no additional benefit. Ultimately, the sample
size must be just right. If you have a sample
size that is too small, your results will not be accurate for
the population. If you have a sample that is too large,
you invest time and resources that you won't get a return on
regarding the accuracy of your results.
