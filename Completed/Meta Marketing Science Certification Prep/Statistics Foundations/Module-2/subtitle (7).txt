learn to recognize four of the common distributions
that describe data. First, we have the
normal distribution. A normal distribution,
often referred to as a bell curve or
Gaussian distribution, is a probability distribution that is symmetric
about the mean, showing that data near
the mean are more frequent in occurrence than
data far from the mean. Normal distributions
are the most common because they occur
naturally all the time. They're shaped like bells and have a few important
characteristics. The mean, median, and mode are all the same value. Also, the curve is symmetrical, so the curve below
or to the left of the mean looks the same as the curve above or to
the right of the mean. You will encounter
this distribution with all kinds of data. For example, in
tracking daily steps, if it is a normal distribution, you'll find that most days cluster around an
average step count, with fewer instances of significantly higher
or lower step counts evenly distributed on either
side of that average. Recognizing normal distributions
is important because numerous statistical tests rely on data fitting this pattern. Using these tests on differently distributed data can lead to incorrect conclusions. Next, we will look at
uniform distributions. A uniform distribution
is a type of probability distribution where all outcomes are equally likely. Each value within
the specified range has an equal chance
of occurring, and the distribution is flat and not skewed in any direction. A uniformed distribution has
a box-like appearance with the horizontal line moving
with the x-axis from the lowest point or
instanced to the highest. For instance, tracking
your daily steps and noticing that your step
count is consistently spread across a specific
range with no days of significantly higher or lower suggest a uniform distribution. Understanding this can help
you assess the consistency of your physical activity and make adjustments as needed for
health or fitness goals. Next, let's explore a
Poisson distribution, which is a little different from a normal or uniform
distribution. A Poisson distribution is
a probability distribution that expresses the probability
of a given number of events occurring in a fixed
interval of time with a known constant mean rate and independently of the time
since the last event. Essentially, it will tell you the probability of something happening based on the number of times that something
else has happened. A Poisson distribution can adopt various shapes and lacks
a unique defining form, like those seen in normal
or uniform distributions. To better understand this distribution,
think of it this way. If event B happens 100 times, what is the probability
of event A happening? Let's use an example. In the Poisson distribution, there's a fairly symmetric shape with a peak of 100 times. On the horizontal axis, you'll notice that the
corresponding percentage is 75%. This means that if event
B happens 100 times, there is a 75% chance
of event A happening. Let's use a different
example to see how this distribution can be applied to a
real-world scenario, this time in the
