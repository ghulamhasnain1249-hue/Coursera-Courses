Planning a party or navigating
rush hour traffic involves predicting patterns just like forecasting stock
trends or energy use. Recognizing these patterns helps us anticipate and
prepare for the future. Data analytics pinpointing
the right distribution type is key to understanding and predicting real world phenomena. In this video, we will explore different
visual features of distributions and how various statistical calculations impact those distributions. A distribution is a
visual representation of the formula that describes the probability of each outcome. Distributions are created from recorded data and used for
the purpose of showing the probability of
any possible outcome occurring within your dataset
using a distribution curve. We've already been working
with some calculations that influence a distribution
like standard deviation. Let's use an example
to understand how to find a distribution and
what features it has. A community center is evaluating which fitness classes to offer based on the interest
of different age groups. You decide to help them by
creating a visualization that shows the ages of members for everyone
taking classes. With this visualization, it's easy to see
that the majority of fitness class attendees are between the ages of 40 and 45. There's still a chance
class attendees will be younger and a chance class
attendees will be older. But the odds are that the next attendee to
enter the class is in the 40-45 age range
based on how high the distribution is with most of the values falling
between 40 and 45. This means that the
community center should offer more
classes that people between the ages of 40 and 45 would enjoy to reach
the largest group. If this concept sounds familiar, then you've been
paying attention. Previously, we discussed
the concept of variance. Variance measures how spread
out your distribution is while distribution predicts the specific probability
of any value, and the two are strongly linked. If you have a high variance, you will have a short
wide distribution because the probability
of the value being around the mean is only a little higher than the probability
of it being anywhere else. If you have a small variance, you will have a tall, narrow distribution
because there is a high probability that any
value will be near the mean. More often than not, distributions will be visualized using a chart
called a histogram. Histograms are a bar chart
with a curve sketched over the bars showing the frequency of values within certain ranges. These ranges can be called bins, buckets, or brackets, but
they all mean the same thing. You now know what
a distribution is, how it relates to variance and how to visualize one
with a histogram. The distribution of
your data will become increasingly important the
further you go in statistics, so getting a firm understanding of it will help you
in the long run.
