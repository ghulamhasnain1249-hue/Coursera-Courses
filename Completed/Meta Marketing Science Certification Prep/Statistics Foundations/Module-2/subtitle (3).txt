Choosing the right sample is crucial for
accurately representing a population, if you were interested in knowing what
soda brand sells the best, you would want to sample people who drink soda and not
people who drink tea or coffee primarily. How you select your sample is important to
make sure you have people with the right qualities, and that they accurately
represent the population as a whole. In this video, we will explore
the different ways to select your sample, the sampling method falls into two main
probability and non-probability sampling. Probability Sampling is a branch
of sample selection that uses random ways to select a group of
people to participate in research. The idea of random refers to making
a choice of selection by chance without any specific pattern,
purpose, bias, or method, in other words, every option has
an equal likelihood of being chosen. In statistics, this often means using
a random number generator to select people from a list, non probability sampling
is a branch of sample selection that uses non random ways to select a group
of people to participate in research. This means participants are chosen
based on specific criteria rather than by chance for this course, we'll be
focusing mainly on probability sampling. The first type of probability
sampling is a simple random sample, a simple random sample is a subset
of a statistical population in which each member of the subset has
an equal probability of being chosen. For example, suppose a gem wants
to survey current members, since they already have the information
for the entire population, selecting a few names using a random number generator
would represent their gem members. If you want to learn more about
the use of random number generators, try checking out the website
random.org dot or searching Google using the keywords
random number generators. The second type of probability
sampling is systematic sampling, a systematic sample is a subset
of a statistical population in which each member is selected
using a fixed periodic interval. Systematic sampling involves selecting
participants from a sequence in a consistent pattern,
such as every fifth or 10th person, it doesn't matter what number
you choose for the interval, but you must stick to it to
achieve a randomized sampling. This method is useful for
populations presented sequentially, for instance, you might survey every
fourth person entering the gym to gauge interest in a weekday
event among Saturday gym goers, ensuring the sampling remains
consistent for randomness. The third type of probability
sampling is stratified sampling, stratified sampling is when the population
is divided into smaller groups, known as strata that share
similar characteristics, then a random sample is taken from
each stratum to ensure the sample accurately reflects
the population's diversity. This method is useful to ensure your
sample reflects the population's makeup, it is used when demographic
proportions are known and you want all groups fairly represented for
instance, if a gems protein powder buyers are 90% male, they're
surveying interest in a new flavor. They risk missing female perspectives
with random sampling due to their smaller numbers, by stratifying they'd sample
90 men and ten women from their buyers, matching the populations gender ratio,
ensuring no group is overlooked. The last type of probability sampling
is cluster sampling, cluster sampling is a method where the population
is divided into separate groups, known as clusters randomly selected
to be included in the study. This method is useful when you're
interested in surveying certain groups, for instance, a gem wants feedback on special deals
from their exercise class participants. Since classes vary,
each acts as a distinct subgroup, in cluster sampling, you'd randomly
select entire classes to survey, Choosing the right sampling method
will depend on the population and the information you want to study. To determine a strong sampling method,
complete a thought exercise on how much each method would work and
how feasible it is. This exercise will help you
select the right sampling method.
