Imagine you're buying
a new home and are using a popular real
estate website that estimates the actual
value of the home based on the average cost of similar
homes in the neighborhood. Did you know that the accuracy
of this estimate can vary significantly whether the
website is using the mean, the median, or mode
for its calculations? Depending on the measure of
central tendency chosen, there could be
unreliable price values. In this video, we'll explore
when to use the measures of central tendency
you learned about earlier; mean, median, and mode to help us find
the middle of a dataset, and we'll do so using this example of homes being
sold in a neighborhood. Let's start by calculating
the mean of this dataset. When adding up these
five home prices and dividing by five, we get a mean of 172,000. But another home in
this neighborhood was recently foreclosed on
and sold for 50,000. Let's see how that
impacts our calculations. We add all of these
numbers together, including the new home sold, $50,000 and get a
total of 910,000. Now, we divide by six, since there are six numbers
in the dataset now and get a mean of $151,666.67. Remember, our mean
before was 172,000, so this is a big difference. The mean is sensitive to very
high or very low values. When you have a dataset with very high or very low values, the mean is not
the best measure. It follows that when there are not high or very low values, the mean can be used as a good measure for the
middle of a dataset. Next, let's see if measuring central tendency
using the median does a better job for us. We find the median of our
new dataset by writing it in order from least
to greatest, 50,000, 125,000, 135,000, 150,000,
200,000, and 250,000. Next, we cross off values one at a time starting
with the lowest, 50,000, and then the
highest, 250,000. Then the next lowest, 125,000 and the next highest, 200,000, leaving us
with two numbers in the middle, 135,000 and 150,000. We have two numbers
in the middle, we will add them to get 285,000, and then divide them by two, giving us a median of 142,500. Our original median was 150,000. So adding the very low $50,000 value did not impact the median as much
as it did the mean. This is because the median
is the better measure to use when a dataset has very
high or very low numbers. Now, when is the mode useful? The mode is useful for
understanding common values in a set like the most
frequent house price in a neighborhood
for home buyers. It's also useful when data isn't numeric such as determining
the most common house color, since you can't calculate the
mean or median for colors. Whether you're
evaluating home prices, assessing salary distributions, or analyzing any other data, choosing the right
statistical approach will guide you to more
reliable conclusions. Keep these concepts
in mind as you encounter data in your
daily life or work, and you'll be better equipped to interpret and use
information effectively.
