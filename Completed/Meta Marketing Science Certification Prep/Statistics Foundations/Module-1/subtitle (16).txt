In this video, we're going
to look at how to create contingency tables
in spreadsheets. A contingency table counts two categorical
variables and puts them into a table format to easily identify overlaps between
the categorical variables. For our dataset, we'll use the status of the home
sale and whether or not the home has over
three bedrooms as our categorical variables to
understand if more homes are pending sale or for sale that have less than three bedrooms or if more homes might
be pending sale or for sales that have
more than three bedrooms. To create our table, we're going to highlight the
data in both columns B and column C. Then we're going
to insert a pivot table. You'll notice because
I highlighted the data before I went to insert
pivot table that the correct data range is identified in this field
of the pivot table. Then I'm going to
select "Create". You'll notice I have a
blank contingency table at this point that I need to tell the spreadsheet
what data to use. We're going to go to rows and we're going to
select Status for our rows and it will populate the three different
statuses for home sales, for sale, pending
sale, and sold. Next, we're going to add the columns as our other
categorical variable. You'll see that there's
an indicator of over three bedrooms
as no or as yes. Last, we're going to add
our values for status. When we add our
values for status, it's already separating out those variables that are
indicating yes or no. So the way we read this table is that for the status for sale, five of the homes in for sale status do not have
over three bedrooms. Three of the homes in pending sale status do not
have over three bedrooms. Nine of the homes in for sale status have
over three bedrooms. Then the grand total column is being calculated
from adding the number of homes in no to the number of homes in yes for
over three bedrooms. You could change this table by switching the rows
and columns differently, but the data would
look the same, and it would be interpreted
the exact same. So the order that
you select the rows or the columns of a
contingency table aren't necessarily
crucially important to differentiate
between when you're trying to make the selection. That is how you make contingency tables
using spreadsheets.
