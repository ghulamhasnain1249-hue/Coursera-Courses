In this video you're going to
learn several ways to measure the central tendency of a data set
in an effort to find its middle. It might seem like only one
value could be the middle. Think of cutting a board in half. The middle is a clear location where
the same measures are measured on both sides of the cut. Measures of central tendency allow
you to find the middle of a data set. This middle, the mean, the median or
the mode is a quick and easy way to summarize a data
set in a single number. That number can be judged on its own or
used to compare two or more data sets. The mean is the calculated
average of a data set. We're most familiar with the mean
as a measure of central tendency since school grades, sports and customer
satisfaction ratings use averages. To get the mean, add all of
the numbers in a data set together and divide by how many numbers there are. Let's look at an example of the mean
in a neighborhood five houses sell for the following prices 150,000,
250,000 ,125, 000, 135, 000 and 200, 000. To find the mean you will first add all of
these numbers together giving you 860,000. Now you need to divide by 5 since there
are five numbers in our data set, this gives us 860,000
divided by 5 giving us 172,000 as our mean or
average for this set of data. Next, let's look at the median. The median is the middle of a data
set which has been sorted from least to greatest. How do we find the median of this set
of data consisting of sold home prices? The first step is to order
the values from least to greatest. Now that our list is sorted,
I have a trick that I like to use for finding the median. I start by crossing out the lowest number
in the data set and then the highest one. I keep doing that,
crossing out the next lowest and the next highest until there's
one value left in the middle. So let's cross off 125, 000 and 250,000. Then lets cross off 135,000 and 200, 000. This leaves us with 150,000 as the only
number in the middle, which is our median. This works perfect for
a dataset with an odd number of values. But what about sets of data that
have an even number of values? Let's add another house into our
data set that sells for 210,000. If we cross them off,
starting with the lowest number 125,000 and then the highest 250,000 and we continue to the next lowest 135,000. And the next highest
210,000 were left with two numbers in the 150,00 and 200,000. When this happens you simply add
the two numbers and divide them by two. So we add 150,000 to
200,000 to get 350,000. Then we divide by two and get 175,000 for
the median of our new data set. The last measure of central
tendency we will review is the mode which is the number
repeated the most in the data set. It is important to realize that
not all datasets have a mode, and some have more than one mode. For example, in our neighborhood
selling prices there is no repeated value so it doesn't have a mode. Now if a house sells for 150,000 and we add it to our existing data set,
150,000 is the mode. If another house sells for 200,000 we now have two values that are repeated two times so
we have two modes, 150,000 and 200,000. You can now find the middle
using the mean, median and mode. The next time you see a statistic that
claims to be the middle or average, consider if you think they use the mean,
median or mode to find that number.
