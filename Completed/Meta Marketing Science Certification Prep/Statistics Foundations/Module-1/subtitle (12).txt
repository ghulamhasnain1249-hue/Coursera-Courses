Context is important
in statistics. For instance, knowing
a player's points per game is useful in a
sport like basketball, but it doesn't directly compare them to
others in the league. That comparison to others
in the league who play on teams with different
dynamics is a key part of understanding whether
a player is exceptional or not when compared to
everyone in the league. In this video, we will
explore Z-scores, which provide us with
a way of leveraging the standard
deviation to fill in the context of where it falls
in reference to the mean. A Z-score explains how
many standard deviations a value is from the mean. The Z-score is a calculated
number that corresponds to the number of
standard deviations a data value is from the mean. In our basketball
player example, this means Z-scores measure how many standard deviations a player's performance is
from the league average. This lets us not just
see how a player scores, but how exceptional
their scoring is compared to their peers. This provides a clear and comparable measure
of performance. But how does a Z-score do this? It starts with a normal
distribution curve or bell curve with the
mean in the middle. Remember, we will convert
the actual data value to a Z-score to understand where it falls on
the distribution. If you have a value
with a Z-score of zero, the value you're comparing
is located at your mean, which is the highest part
of the distribution. The Z-score tells
us the location of the original value relative
to all the other data. In this case, the location in the middle at the mean shows the original raw
score is bigger than 50% of all your data points
because it is in the middle. It's also smaller than
50% of your data as well. Let's do another example. Now, the Z-score is
calculated to equal one, which means the value is one standard deviation away from the mean on the
right-hand side. We know it is on
the right-hand side because it is a positive one. If it had been negative one, it would be one standard
deviation below the mean. The negative would
indicate below, which is to the
left of the mean. In order to help you more
accurately calculate Z-scores, you can use a Z-score table, which is a list of standardized
calculations to help you understand exactly
what percentage of your population falls
below your Z-score. These tables are not
as common today as statistical software is often used to find a Z-score
and probability. Let's examine a specific
example from Inu and Neko to understand how Z-scores can be applied
to a marketing effort. Carlos at Inu and Neko, would like to launch a product
targeted at older pets. Carlos already knows that his customer's pets average age or mean age is
eight years old. But Carlos wants
to launch products for pets ages 14
years and older. He's wondering if that
age is close enough to the mean to keep the
current market strategy, or if he needs to create a new marketing strategy
for this new product. Is 14 years too old for the
current advertising campaign? Let's find out using a Z-score. First, we'll use statistical software to
find the Z-score of three, which means that it is three standard deviations
from the mean. We use statistical software
to find the exact percentage, and the answer is
0.9987 or 99.87%. That means that 14
years old is older than 99.87% of Inu and Neko's
current pet base. It is safe to say that
a new campaign strategy would be required to market to this group of pets
and their owners. If the new product were aimed at pets who
are four years old, the Z-score would be -2. That means that four is two standard deviations
lower than the mean. If we check this on a Z-score
in statistics software, we will find a probability
of 0.0228 or 2.28%. Four years old is only older
than 2.28% of the pets. Conversely, it means
it is younger than 97.72% of the customer base. For this pet population, as well as the pets
over 14 years old, Carlos would likely adopt a new marketing strategy to
better target this audience. By understanding how Z-scores provide context to raw data, such as the basketball
players points per game, we can assess their
performance in relation to the league
average more effectively. Z-scores give us a way to view individual data points in the context of a
larger data set. As you continue to learn
