from anyone seeking answers, including academics, business leaders,
healthcare professionals, a client, or even government officials,

exploring new knowledge, solving specific challenges,

up with questions, how do you, as a data analyst, refine a question
to be a good research question? Let's explore some of the qualities
of a good research question. First, be specific. Broad questions can be
challenging to address. For instance, wondering, will I be
healthier this year, is too vague. Instead, break it down into
manageable questions like how many times a week should I
exercise to improve my stamina? Or what dietary changes can
lower my cholesterol this year? The clearer and more precise your
questions, the easier they are to answer. Second, ensure your research
question aligns with your goals. For instance, finding a link
between types of exercise and life satisfaction is interesting, but
if you aim to lower health risks, it doesn't directly align with your goals. Your question should directly
align with improving your health. Last check if you have
the resources to obtain new data or must use existing health datasets,
know what information and variables you can use as asking
questions your data cannot answer means you can't conduct your study. Instead, work within the resources
available to you and be a creative thinker in
obtaining the data you need. Let's evaluate an example question and
see if it meets all of these criteria. In our health example, lets assume
that the goal is to study factors that can lower blood pressure over time. The question, does incorporating
30 minutes of cardiovascular exercise into my daily routine decrease
my blood pressure over six months, was proposed to guide the study. Does it meet the criteria? For a good research question? Let's find out. This question is specific because it
targets a particular type of exercise, cardiovascular, and a defined duration,
30 minutes daily, and a clear outcome, decrease in blood pressure,
within a set time frame of six months. It avoids vagueness and
provides a clear focus for the study. The question aligns with the goal
of studying ways to lower blood pressure over time. It focuses on a lifestyle change
daily cardiovascular exercise and its effects on blood pressure,
a key factor in many health issues. This question requires measuring
blood pressure accurately using personal monitors or
healthcare services over time. It's possible you or
your team can monitor these metrics for six months using a blood pressure monitor,
which is low cost and could even transmit data directly
from a participant to your team. Overall, this question meets the criteria. It's specific, aligns with health goals,
and is achievable given resource limits, making it a solid choice for
health research. Asking a question might seem
