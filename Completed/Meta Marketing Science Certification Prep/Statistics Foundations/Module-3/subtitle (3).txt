and observational. No matter how complicated, all studies fall into one of two categories, observational
or experimental. Simply put, observational
studies are where you look at or observe what is happening naturally without
trying to influence it. There are many different types
of observational studies. Let's explore a couple
of the most common ones. Cohort studies track a
group of people over time. Researchers identify
a group with a common characteristic and
observe them over time to see if exposure to a factor
like smoking is linked to an outcome like lung cancer without
assigning any interventions. Another type of observational
study is a case study. In case studies, researchers compare people with
a specific outcome, cases to those
without it controls, looking back to identify
differences and exposures or risk factors
between the groups. For a study on workplace
productivity, for example, researchers could
compare employees who use standing desks, cases with those who use
traditional desks controls. Examining past performance
records to see if desk type influences
productivity. Another common form of observational study
is data mining. Data mining is the process of extracting insights
from a large database. The idea is that you
already have all the data collected and stored and are just trying to
find meaning in it. For data mining, a
company might analyze transaction records to
identify buying patterns, trying to reveal which products customers frequently
purchase together. Let's now explore
the other category of studies, the
experimental study. An experimental study is a
study in which a treatment, procedure or program
is intentionally introduced and a result
or outcome is observed. In other words, an
experimental study, you change something,
the treatment to see if it influences
the results. There are countless types
of experimental studies, but here are a few of
the most common ones. Repeated measures
involves taking multiple measurements
in a study. For instance, if
you give a survey, show a participant a video and then give them
a survey again, you can see if the video
changed their opinion. This type of study has many
different forms and names, but it all amounts to the same basic
concept of measuring, influencing, and then
measuring again. AB testing is also a type
of experimental study. AB testing splits
participants into two random groups to
compare treatments A and B. For example, with two
online ad versions, A and B, you can
observe and track how reactions differ
between the two groups exposed to each version. One of the most common
experimental study types is the randomized control trial where participants are
divided into two groups. One receives a treatment, and the other, known as the
control group, does not. This control group serves as
the baseline for comparison. For instance, in a study, you might have two groups. One simply takes a
survey, the control, while the other
watches an ad before taking the same survey,
the treatment group. Comparing the two allows you to assess the ads
impact on opinions. Let's use the
