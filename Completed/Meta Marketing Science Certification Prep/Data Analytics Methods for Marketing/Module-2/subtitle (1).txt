We're ready to get started
with this week's lesson. We'll start by talking about
the sales funnel as it is the backbone of all the metrics we're discussing in this lesson. A sales funnel, also called a purchase funnel
or revenue funnel, is a way to visualize your potential customer's
first interaction with your brand all the way
through to repeat purchase. The sales funnel is shaped
like an inverted pyramid because there are more users at the top of the
funnel than the bottom. There are different
versions of the funnel, but the basic structure
is as follows. The top of the funnel is
often labeled as awareness. This should indicate the user is aware of the brand or product. Next is interest,
understanding or discovery. The mid funnel is often
the hardest to quantify, but it should
indicate the user is interested in or understands
the brand or product. The third stage of the funnel is generally called decision
or consideration. This is where the user considers their options and begins
to make a decision. Next is conversion or sales, where a user actually purchases. After sales comes
loyalty or repurchase, which may come in the form of multiple purchases
or a referral. It's important to note the
B2B and B2C funnel may look different as B2B often has a longer lead time and
may include more steps. The funnel is important because it gives
marketers a way to visualize the journey
a customer takes on the way to purchase
and repurchase. The marketer's goal is
twofold: on a micro level, moving an individual user
from one stage to the next; and on a macro level, moving as many people
as possible through the funnel by increasing
conversion rates at each step. Marketers can use the funnel
to optimize each step of the funnel from
click-through rate to lead rate to conversion rate. As we talk about
return on investment, return on ad spend, and customer profit, you'll want to keep
the funnel in mind. How can we optimize
the purchase funnel to attract more customers
and have them repurchase? In our second video, we'll look at descriptive
versus predictive metrics, understanding what
each is good for and why they're both valuable
in different ways. Then we'll explore
the importance of and the difference between
return on ad spend, return on investment,
and customer profit. We'll shift gears in

like return on ad spend, return on investment,
