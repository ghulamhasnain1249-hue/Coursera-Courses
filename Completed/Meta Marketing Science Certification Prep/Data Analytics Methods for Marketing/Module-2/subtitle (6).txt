You've previously learned
about customer profitability, the backwards-looking
model that reviews just how profitable a customer
has been to a company. Now we want to focus on a forward-looking
predictive model called Customer Lifetime Value, sometimes abbreviated
CLTV or just LTV. Customer lifetime value is the expected profitability
of a consumer and is forward-looking rather
than just an analysis of the current data to date,
like customer profitability. Why use CLTV if we've already calculated
customer profitability? CLTV assumes that customers
can or will change over time, whereas customer
profitability only looks at how a customer
has acted to date. Above all, CLTV incorporates
forward-looking data to, hopefully, make a more
accurate prediction of what the customer
is really worth. We'll talk about
the actual formula for CLTV in an upcoming video. But first, let's ask ourselves
why this metric matters. First, having a lifetime
value of a consumer allows us to focus on not just
the cheapest acquisition or lowest CAC, and instead focus on acquiring long-term,
profitable customers. In other words, it may be okay to spend a little more
money on acquiring a customer that you
expect to generate a lot of value for the
company over time. You may know it's more efficient for a
business to nurture current relationships versus
acquiring new customers. But did you know
that research done by Frederick Reichheld
of Bain & Company, shows that increasing
customer retention rates by five percent increases
profits by 25-95 percent? Second, calculating CLTV helps
our marketing efforts by helping us to segment out in new ways and target
those customers. Do we want to focus on those
already with high CLTV? What about those
that are showing increased propensity to
buy but moderate CLTV? Finally, having a customer
lifetime value can also lead to increased
ROI. How so? By focusing on those customers that are likely to
purchase again and often versus those who only purchase once or only
wait for steep discounts. As you can see, there are many reasons to calculate
customer lifetime value. In our next videos, we'll focus on how to calculate lifetime
value and apply it to a company so you can
see CLTV in action.
