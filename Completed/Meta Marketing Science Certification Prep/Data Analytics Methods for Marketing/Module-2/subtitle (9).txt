this is linear regression. Linear regression is
an analysis that shows the relationship between an independent and a
dependent variable. In marketing, we may use linear regression to find
answers to questions like, does click-through rate on social ads have an
effect on conversion? Does video completion rates have an effect on
site visitation? Do sales go up when we
spent more on Facebook? In our first question, the independent variable is click-through rate and the dependent variable
is conversions. In the second question, completion rate is our independent variable
and site visitation is our dependent variable. Linear regression can
help us understand these relationships and predict outcomes based on
that understanding. Let's walk through the formula for linear regression together. We looked at this before in a previous course
in this program. The formula for linear
regression is y equals b times x plus a. y is
the dependent variable, b is the slope of
the regression line, x is the independent variable, and a is the y-intercept
of the regression line. The slope of the
regression line, or b in this formula is
crucial in this formula as it tells us how much our dependent variable
changes with the change in x. In other words,
it's the ratio of change in y for a change in x. You could consider
it an indication of how much x and y are correlated. Note that b could be negative. In that case, a positive
change in x would result in a negative
change in y or vice versa. It means the independent
and dependent variables move in opposite directions. The y-intercept is another
part of the formula. Here, represented as a. It specifies where
the regression line intercepts the
y-axis in the plot. While an important
part of the formula, there isn't always
a meaningful way to interpret this point. Let's look at an example of how regression analysis
