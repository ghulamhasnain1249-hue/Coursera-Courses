While there are a few
different kinds of analytics, we want to hone in
on two kinds to understand how they
help our business. Descriptive Analytics
look at data in the past to explain
what happened; while Predictive
Analytics look to explain what could
happen in the future. It's important to note, one is not more important than the others, but
they're complimentary. Descriptive Analytics is
the most commonly used form of analytics where
data is collected, analyzed, and presented in a
way humans can understand. This is where our pie charts, line graphs, and
scatterplots come into play. You would use
Descriptive Analytics to answer questions like, what's our
year-over-year revenue? How has inventory moved
in the last 12 months? How many people have we hired
in the last six months? What's the engagement rate
of our Facebook page? Descriptive Analytics
can be incredibly beneficial to a business as
it tells you what happened. However, it doesn't tell you what could happen
moving forward. That's where Predictive
Analytics come in. Predictive Analytics
can't tell the future, but it can tell you
what's likely to happen. It uses probability to say what's most likely to
happen by looking at the data and filling
in the blanks using methods like machine
learning and deep learning. Predictive Analytics can be used to answer questions like, what are expected sales
in the next 12 months? How much revenue should we expect from this
marketing campaign? What will the demand for our
product be next Christmas? How much less overtime will we have if we scale up the team? Predictive Analytics
can be used for many things including:
improving business efficiency, staffing, fraud
detection, risk analysis, marketing efforts,
and sales planning. Now that we know the
difference between Descriptive and
Predictive Analytics, we will dive into
the world of ROI, ROAS, and CP. If you don't know
these acronyms yet, don't worry, you'll learn
them in our next videos.
