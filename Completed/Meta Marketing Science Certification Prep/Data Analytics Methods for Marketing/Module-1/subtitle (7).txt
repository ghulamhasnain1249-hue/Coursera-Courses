A strong segmentation strategy is just
one piece of the marketing puzzle. We can also use statistics and
algorithms to inform creative decisions. In this video, I will give you an overview
of the different methods that influence the ads and marketing messages we see. Did you know that according to Infogroup,
over 90% of consumers say that messages from companies that are not
personally relevant to them are annoying. Good ads are a huge part
of that experience. So how do statistics and
algorithms inform good ads? Personalization enables advertisers to
reach users based on their interests and demographics. There's no one tactic that
defines personalization. You may recognize it when a company
uses your name in the subject line for an email for instance. But personalization goes
much further than that. There are four key kinds
of personalization. Wisdom of the crowd,
semantic understanding, segmentation and targeting and one to one personalization. Wisdom of the crowd is
focused on finding items and ads you might like if others who
look like you also like them. This is often how companies begin
personalization at scale as it moves their advertising from one to many,
to one to few. So, instead of just targeting
everyone with a message, only a few people are targeted
because you have reason to believe that they will be
more interested in your message. Let's go back to James at SnackWall. Remember, James is trying to sell snack
subscriptions to businesses who would like a convenient snack option in the office. James creates an app based audience
list to develop a list of previous purchasers from the SnackWall app for
Facebook advertising. Facebook that looks at his data for
correlations and finds users who purchased crackers also more often purchase
cheese than a typical user. So as a result, the next time a user
who purchased crackers appears on the Facebook platform,
they are served an ad for cheese snacks. Semantic understanding is used
to understand the context behind the words on a page. This is most often used in search but
it can also be used in contextual advertising to serve a relevant ad to
a user on a contextually relevant page. For example,
if James has an ad focused on sports and wants to serve this in sports articles,
a natural language processing algorithm would parse an article to say
yes this is contextually relevant and it's about sports and
serve SnackWall ad. What's important about semantic
understanding is that if given enough data and algorithm will be able
to tell the difference between polar bears versus the Chicago
bears football team for instance. Segmentation and targeting is the backbone
of many algorithmic optimization and personalization techniques. Say SnackWall has three Facebook
campaigns running each targeted to a different snack segment. One campaign is targeted to focus tech
audiences want to sports enthusiasts and want to healthy foods audiences. While James has creative themes for each, he decides to run all creative for
each audience to let the Facebook algorithm decides to show which
creative is performing best. To his surprise,
the Facebook algorithm is serving more sports creative to
the healthy foods audience. This is the power of segmentation and
targeting. While James assumed healthy food
creative would work best for the healthy foods audience. The Facebook algorithm notices that users
who fit the segment profile of a healthy food consumers are more likely to purchase
after seeing a sports creative message. Last but certainly not least
is one to one personalization. This approach looks at individual
characteristics to deliver a unique experience to a user. This could be the way
a website is laid out and ad you are served or
even your search results. While many companies strive for one to one personalization can
be very hard to do it at scale. One to one personalization looks at
everything an algorithm knows about you to predict your behavior and serve you the most ideal
experience to drive that behavior. As an example, if you head to search
engine and look up baseball tickets while you are in New York, you may receive
search results for New York teams. If you do the exact same
search in san Francisco, you may receive different search results. Further, you may receive different
search results if you've previously shown an interest in baseball
versus someone who never has. One to one personalization looks at
all of the elements a company knows about a consumer in order to provide
the best experience possible. To really up your personalization game, the four types of personalization
can feed into dynamic creative. Dynamic creative can change
elements including, but not limited to the primary text,
image, headline and description in real time given what we know about to
use it and the context on the page. Dynamic creative is available for
many creative types, including social posts,
display banners and video. Let's go back to James at SnackWall. Remember he saw that the sports
themed creative performs best for his healthy foods audience. James decides to expand his creative
strategy to run dynamic creative on Facebook. Each ad has three different
forms of primary text available, one with buy one get one free,
one with no offer and one with 50% off. Facebook then looks at each user
individually within their segments to decide what image and
primary text they would serve. With their dynamic creative
optimization to our sales, SnackWall sees their subscriptions
increase 77% over the next two months. Personalization in advertising
is very important. It is what makes digital advertising
more effective and it allows us to put the right message in front of
the right audience at the right time. Personalization is data-driven. So it's important to have a structured
way to keep and access your data. Let's look at that in our next video.
