type of segmentation called clustering. Analyst Nikita Sharma defined clustering
in a blog post she wrote on Heartbeat. Clustering is the process of dividing the
data space or data points into a number of groups, such that data points in the
same groups are more similar to other data points in the same group, and dissimilar
to the data points in other groups. But, more simply, it's about finding
similarities in the data and grouping data points
by these similarities. Clustering is a predictive
form of segmentation. Clustering identifies what
people do most of the time, in order to predict what customers
are most likely to do, moving forward. It finds the similarities so
they can then be segmented. For example, let's say SnackWall,
our snack subscription service has purchased their data on business size and
dollar amount of orders per year. They then use clustering to look
through the data for clusters, grouping these data points together. James at SnackWall is surprised to
find a cluster of small businesses who ordered very often and decides to segment them out into their own
campaign of high lifetime value users. Well this may be easy to spot by looking
at the data plot in this example, clustering can find the patterns in the
data that we might not see on the surface. It's important to test
different numbers of clusters, to see the similarity among them. Too few clusters can mean
you're being too general, but too many clusters can
cause too much noise. Think of it this way, if you take 100
people and ask them to choose between two numbers, 1 and 2, you'll wind up
with two large audiences that may not share many things in common, and
in turn, may not be very effective. On the other hand, if you ask those 100
people to pick their favorite number, without limiting their options, you may end up with a bunch of very small
audiences that may not be very useful. The key is to figure out how many
clusters give you enough granularity while still being actionable. We'll discuss this more as we move
forward and dive into k-means clustering. Let's take a look at a real-life
