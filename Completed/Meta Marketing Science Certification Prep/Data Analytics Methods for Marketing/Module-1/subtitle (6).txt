Let's go back to Snackwall. James has decided to target the focused tech segment for his campaign. But what does that actually look like from a segmentation perspective? In this case, focused tech is the persona. But we need to begin segmenting this persona in order to eventually find and target this audience. Segmentation variables come in four main forms. Demographic, psychographic, geographic and behavioral. Demographic segmentation includes factors like age, gender, marital status, income and education level. We're trying to reach a younger, more tech-focused audience, so age is a factor here. One segmentation point will be ages 25 to 34. Next, we want to consider psychographic behaviors. These include things like values, motivations and beliefs. These variables are often harder to determine because we don't always know our audience's inner thoughts. James has done some survey research on this tech-savvy audience and knows that they consider the latest and greatest technology to be important in their lives. Great, another segmentation point. We also need to think about geographic considerations. James does not have enough data to know if geography plays a huge factor yet, but he'd like to slightly focus on tech-friendly US cities like San Francisco, New York, Austin and Miami. This will be helpful when he's planning budgets and running campaigns later. Last, we have behavioral variables. Behavioral variables come in two forms. The first is the actual relationship the user has with the product, including usage, spending habits and the perceived benefits of using the product. In our case, this is a new segment for SnackWall, so they do not have that data on this yet. The second form of behavioral variables are the actual behaviors of the user themselves. For instance, you may want to focus on tech usage, media consumption or spending habits. This focused tech audience is tech-savvy. There's another segmentation point. What about their media habits? You decide to do some research and find they are more active on Twitter and LinkedIn. Great, there's another segmentation point. Since we're discussing technology, it's important to keep in mind things like operating systems. Since this audience is tech-minded, they are most likely running the most recent operating systems on Android or iOS, so let's use that in our segmentation too. You can see how we've taken a persona like focused tech and parsed it into segments, so we're then able to find these users in digital marketing. But remember, there's a second way we can segment using clustering in our own data. Let's look at another example from a hypothetical company, FocusVu. FocusVu is a video conferencing company that sells online video conferencing solutions for individuals and businesses. They have three subscription tiers, free, personal, and business. FocusVu wants to get as many users into their business tier pricing as possible. They decide to segment out personal tier brand loyalists who may be interested in switching to the business tier. In order to do this, they segment out the top personal tier users by looking at how long a user has been a FocusVu member and how much they use the platform. Then, FocusVu targets them with an ad campaign on Facebook, urging them to upgrade to the business tier. This is a basic example of segmentation using customer data. We'll get into more complex methods throughout this course. Let's look at a real-life case study from MetLife Insurance written up by Harvard Business School's student, R Waitumbi. MetLife was challenged by technological innovations in the marketplace, and newcomers like Lemonade threatened to steal customers. In addition to outside forces, customers were expecting more from the brand than ever. This combination of factors spurred MetLife to try to find a competitive advantage in this space through the use of segmentation. After interviewing and surveying more than 50,000 customers, MetLife developed five key segments using cluster analysis that went beyond their typical demographic information they had used in the past. As you can see, MetLife focused on demographic insights like age and gender, as well as behavioral factors like tech usage and media consumption. MetLife also looked at psychographic elements, like a segment's price sensitivity and confidence about financial matters. MetLife then used this segmentation strategy to educate their Salesforce and corporate customers, encouraging them to think about their employees through a combination of demographic and psychographic data. Segmentation can be a powerful tool in a marketer's arsenal. Using segmentation helps us better understand our audience and plan our marketing in a smarter way.
