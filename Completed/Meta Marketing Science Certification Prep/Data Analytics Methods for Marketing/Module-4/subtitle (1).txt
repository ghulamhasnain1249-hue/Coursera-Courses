So far, we've looked at using
statistical modeling for a number of individual
marketing questions, like which audience is likely
to buy a specific product or what's the impact of a certain
website design on sales or how much ad spent should I budget
based on past performance and how does a certain banner ad placement
affect click through rate as a marketer. You certainly want to understand how each
individual action impacts your brand awareness conversions and sales. But what about the big picture when you
are working on your marketing strategy and planning the budget, you should spend,
you will want to get more insight into the impact of all your individual
marketing initiatives working together. Marketing mix modeling can help with that. Similar to all the other
analysis we've seen, you would gather the data from each
of your marketing initiatives. Then you would apply an analysis
that looks at all that data and finally gives you a model that shows
the combined impact your marketing efforts have on your sales and revenue. In other words, it gives you a model
that shows you what's working and what's not, that's how it gets its name. You're not just looking at one
marketing channel or initiative, but a mix of them to see which
strategies work and which don't. Knowing which strategies are the most
effective and which generate most sales means that you can focus on those
strategies in your marketing efforts and do away with or rethink
the strategies that aren't working and you can rebalance your
budgets to reflect that, let's take another look at our
flower shop Calla and Ivy. To see how they might use
marketing mix modeling Imra at Calla and Ivy has reached the point where
she has been in business for a few years and she wants to gain
a greater insight into how her marketing efforts have impacted her
sales over that time. She's used a mix of marketing efforts and
as she thinks about how to budget for marketing in the future, she wants to make sure her money is
spent as effectively as possible. She's already tracked a number of
individual marketing efforts and their impact on her sales. For example, she ran a Facebook campaign
last holiday Season during which online orders increased by 10%. In this past Mother's Day, she took out a series of print ads during
which in store sales bumped $5,000. Camera could assume that the 10%
increase in online sales is directly attributed to the Facebook ad Campaign and
the 5000 extra dollars in in store sales is directly attributed to
the print ads, but other Calla and ivy marketing initiatives were
happening during that time as well. In addition to Facebook ads at holidays,
Imra was doing a lot of organic engagement on social media and in addition
to the print ads around Mother's Day, Imra did a Facebook live session to
talk about planning flower purchases. She also sponsored the post on Instagram
in partnership with another local shop. So which efforts actually
resulted in those sales if it was the Facebook campaign and the print
ads that directly caused those sales, then Imra will know that those
investments were money well spent. But what if it was the unpaid organic
engagement on social media that really led to those sales,
then Imra knows she can save money in the future by investing time and
creativity into organic channels. But what if it was the sponsored posts on
Instagram that actually led to the sales? Or what if it was a combination of all of
these initiatives that led to these sales? How would she know ? Marketing Mixed Model will tell us the answer, let's get started on learning
more about how this works.
