So far, you've learned about the important outcome
variables marketers focus on and how
to calculate them. We saw how to find segments to help us determine
our target audience, and you learned how you can use regression to help you
plan and forecast. We also covered the
different ways in which you can analyze
the effectiveness of a campaign using observational or
experimental methods. But as you can imagine, marketers need to look
beyond one campaign and they often juggle different campaigns and actions on
different platforms. It's great to evaluate
the effectiveness of each of those
actions individually. But you also want to understand
how all these actions and efforts work together to ultimately help you
reach your goal. Which of your ads was
most influential? Where should you allocate your precious marketing dollars? Which parts of the sales funnel are affected by my actions? All of these are very common and
important questions that marketing analysts
help to answer. This week, you'll learn about different optimization
techniques and you'll finish with a new
set of tools that you can apply in your
role as an analyst. In this first lesson, we're going to focus on
marketing mix modeling, a technique that
brings historical data from marketing actions in
different channels together, and through iterative modeling, helps you understand how much each of them influence
your results. Then we'll look at attribution, which also looks
across channels, but it's often used to evaluate
digital channels only. Attribution analysis is a
bit more immediate than marketing mix modeling and uses a different approach
to building a model. Finally, we'll look at how you can analyze
your sales funnel to understand how your company is performing at the awareness, consideration, and
conversion stage and what that could mean for
your marketing optimization. Another full week, so
let's get started.
