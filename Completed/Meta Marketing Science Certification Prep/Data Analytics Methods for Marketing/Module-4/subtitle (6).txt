data input and apply a model to that data. The model we use is determined
before we run the analysis. There are two different types of models
that are commonly used single-touch and multi-touch models. Will explain the most common
single-touch models in this video. In single-touch attribution
models the assumption is that there is a single ad that is
responsible for the results you observe. So even if a person is exposed to
many ads for a particular product or service before they decide to take action,
the action like a purchase for instance is attributed to
just one of those ads. The most commonly used model is
the last click attribution model. It gives 100% credit to the last click
from a person before a conversion took place. Even if a person saw and clicked on many
ads before that the model still assumes that the last add the person clicked
on is solely responsible for the sale. Let's think about an example,
remember Kathy from our last video, she was looking for a mattress. And on her journey before
buying the mattress, she was exposed to many different ads. If we were to use the last
click attribution model, we would assume that the last ad,
Kathy clicked on was 100% responsible for the mattress purchase she made. So even though Kathy saw and maybe
even interacted with some ads before, only last ad she clicked on would be
considered to be the reason she purchased. In this case,
that was an ad she saw on her computer. Another single-touch attribution
model is the last touch model. Like last click models, this model
also takes only one touch point into consideration, but in this case,
there is no need for a click. This model gives 100% credit to
the last ad a person interacted with before a conversion, whether the person
just saw the ad or clicked on it. So in our example for Kathy the ad that would get the credit
would be the very last ad Kathy saw or clicked on, which would be the ad
she saw in the New York Times. Then there's the first click attribution
model which gives 100% credit for a conversion to the first
click on the conversion path. So in Kathy's case we would attribute her
mattress purchase to the first ad she clicked on, which just happens to be
the same single ad she clicked on. So in this case, the last click or the first click attribution model
would give us the same result. Finally, the first touch attribution
model which gives 100% credit for a conversion to the first
interaction in the conversion path, whether that interaction was a view or
a click. So basically, all credit goes to
the first ad the person sees or clicks. In Kathy's case all the credit for her
purchase would go to the very first ad she saw for a purple mattress
on her Facebook news feed. So now we know the four single
touch attribution models. But you may wonder how marketers
actually use these models or more importantly how they
decide which model to use. Having an attribution model will help
marketers decide on how to plan for their marketing mix for
future campaigns, or how to optimize campaigns
that are running now. If we think that the last ad
people interacted with is entirely responsible for their purchase,
like in the last touch model, and if we see the last ad was most of
the time a Google search ad for instance, then advertisers may decide to shift
more of their budget to Google search. As they consider it responsible for
most actions. So you can see how the type of attribution
model you choose has a big effect on your budget planning. So how do you choose the type
of attribution model then? Much depends on the type of business
you have or your business goal. If you use first touch or first click
attribution, you put a lot of weight on the beginning of the customer journey or
the top of the purchase funnel. In fact, you're saying that the ad that
helped generate awareness was responsible for the purchase you're seeing. With a model like that you would recommend
to shift more of your marketing budget to advertising, that helps to add
people to the top of the funnel. Marketers may like that strategy, if it's
their goal to generate more awareness for a brand or a product, like in the case
of a new product launch, for instance. With last click or
last touch attribution models, a lot more weight is given
to the bottom of the funnel. In other words, you really credit the ad
that is responsible for closing the deal. In cases where you're eager to
move more people to conversion, like in direct response campaigns,
this may be a better strategy to use. But while these single
touch models are popular, marketers agree that they're not a very
accurate representation of reality. For the majority of conversions,
it takes more than one touch point for people to convert. Hence, models that give credit
to more than one touch points are considered to be more accurate
when evaluating what made people buy. Models that credit more than one
touch points are referred to as multi-touch attribution models. Let's look at those in our next video.
