Now that we've learned
about optimizing sales funnels on
a tactical level, let's zoom out to think more strategically about
sales funnel shapes. Visualizing the funnel
is a great way to understand where
your opportunities are with your customers, too many people
staying in awareness or too few customers
in conversion. Visualizing the funnel
will let you know where customers are and what
messaging they may need. A healthy funnel is sometimes
called the classic funnel. This funnel has people
continually coming into the awareness phase with some customers going
down into consideration, then fewer going
into evaluation, then even fewer going
into conversion. If you see this funnel
shape, congratulations. Your funnel is very
likely healthy. To stay healthy, this funnel should
be continually refreshed with new users
in the awareness stage. Keep an eye on your funnel shape to ensure it stays the same. It's very common to see the classic funnel shape narrow considerably
at the bottom. In that case, people come
in at the awareness stage, and they may show some
level of interest, but they don't make it all
the way through to purchase. That's when you should go
back to your checklist and explore what's happening
during the checkout process. Is there anything in
your website that's blocking people
from checking out? Are they leaving when you require them to give their
personal information? Maybe you're asking
them to sign in, and that complicates things. Are the costs too high? Maybe shipping is
an issue and so on. In a case like this, it may be worth considering a conversion campaign where you specifically target
people who have had an interaction with
your brand already, and encourage them to convert, for instance, better promotion. As you examine
your sales funnel, you may run into another shape, sometimes called the blockage. This is where a
majority of users are stuck into consideration
or evaluation stage, but your awareness and
conversion numbers are low. If you see this, consider why consumers may
be staying in that phase. If you're B2C, take a look at your
product detail pages and check out flow. Is it easy for
people to go through the conversion phase or
are there obstacles? If you're B2B, take a look
at your lead gen process. Perhaps you're selling
too soon or haven't provided enough information
for users to convert, and you may be attracting
the wrong audience. In a situation like this, you may need to consider another awareness campaign so you can attract more people
to the top of the funnel. But you should pay special attention to
your target audience. It may be a good idea to get more insight into the
people who do convert and try to mimic their
characteristics in your target audience for
your awareness campaign. You could combine this with
a conversion campaign, targeting people
who have interacted with your brand to
try to convert them. The last shape we can look for is the reverse funnel shape. This reverse funnel has very little activity in the
awareness, consideration, and evaluation stages, and large swaths of users
in the conversion stage. While this may seem
great at first, a reverse funnel can lead to a lack of customers
down the road. If you see this shape, whether you are a B2B or B2C, you'll want to focus on
your awareness tactics to bring more users into
your sales process. Analyzing your sales funnel
shape can be a great way to develop a holistic strategy towards your media campaigns, and help you understand where you should be
focusing your time. Once you have the shape down, you'll be able to deep dive
into the questions previously discussed in order to optimize your sales
funnel for success. Next, let's take a
