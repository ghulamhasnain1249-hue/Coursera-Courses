a time called a variable, the farmers were able to pinpoint which
factor yielded the largest benefit. Imagine you have two plants and
your goal is to increase planned growth. You want to test the hypothesis that
giving plants fertilizer increases growth. In other words growth is the dependent
variable you're interested in and fertilizer is the independent variable or the variable you believe
will affect growth. You can call one of your plans plant A and
the other one is plant B. You give fertilizer to plan B and
not to plant A. A few weeks later you
measure your plants and you see that plant be
grew more than plant A. Since the conditions your plants grew
in where otherwise exactly the same, you know that fertilizer must
have cost plan B's growth. This is the foundation of
experimental design methodology, you treat one group of subjects and
you don't treat the other group. You measure the differences in results
between both groups after treatment and learn about the effect of your treatment. If you keep the conditions for
both groups exactly the same and only vary one factor or variable
like the fertilizer in our example, you can assign the results you're
seeing to that one variable. We call the group that received
the treatment the treatment or test group, and the group that you didn't
treat the control group. The one variable that differs between
the groups is often referred to as the treatment or
the treatment variable. So how do experimental design
principles apply to measuring advertising effectiveness? In advertising your treatment could be
exposing someone to a particular ad, so you can use experiments to measure
the effects of your ad on the results you're hoping to achieve. For example,
if one group sees your ads and another group doesn't see your ads,
you can measure how many results or sales you achieve from the group that saw
your ads and compare that to the number of results or sales you see from
the group that didn't see your ads. The difference in results or sales can then be considered
the results of your advertising. But as I'm sure you can imagine creating
