of measuring the effect of advertising. As the name suggests, in these methods,
you run an experiment. That basically means that you carefully
plan ahead of time that some people will be exposed to your ads and
some people won't see your ads at all. At the end of your advertising campaign, you would then measure the results,
usually sales for each group. The people who did and
the people who did not see that ad. If all else is equal for both groups,
then it's fair to say that the difference you see between
the group that saw your ads and the group that did not see your ads is
a result of your advertising campaign. Therefore, the incremental result
in the group that was exposed to your ad is considered a result
of your advertising and thus shows you
the effectiveness of your ads. Experiments take careful planning and they have to be entirely set up
before you start running a campaign. They are used for many different types of
campaigns, both digital and non digital. But to execute experiments
correctly is quite complex. In order for you to conclude
that your ad was responsible for an increase in sales you see in
the group that was exposed to your ad, you need to control all other variables. So that both groups were
indifferent in any other way than their exposure to your ad. For instance,
in traditional TV advertising, you might decide to show an ad for your
product in one region of the country, but not in another similar region. Then you can evaluate
the resulting increase in sales for the region where people
were exposed to your ad. That's a good way to test
the effect of your ad. But it's not a pure experiment, as there
may be other factors that may differ for people in the different regions that
may also have an effect on sales. Pure experimental design
is hard to achieve, but there are ways to get
as close as possible. We'll talk more about

in a situation where they can't use experiments to evaluate
the effectiveness of advertising. They may not have the luxury to isolate
two different groups in the audience they're targeting because of
budget constraints, for instance. Or they may be asked to evaluate
the effectiveness of advertising after the campaigns have already run, so they won't have a chance to
carefully designed an experiment. That's when analysts will rely on
observational methods instead. In observational methods,
researchers collect information based on what they observe and
infer insights based on that information. The researcher has no control
over the subjects or variables. In other words,
the researcher doesn't interfere or plan ahead who will and
won't be exposed to a campaign. That's how observational methods
are different from experiments because they observe the impact
of marketing action or advertisement without controlling
who is or isn't exposed to it. In observational methods, researchers will also compare exposed and
non exposed groups. But whereas in the experimental methods
groups are as alike as possible. For example in age, gender, or other attributes because
the researcher designed it that way. In the observational methods, there's
no guarantee that the groups are alike. In observational methods,
marketers have less control. They have to work with the available
