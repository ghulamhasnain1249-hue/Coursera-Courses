When the poll closed on
the evening of 1977 election, the chief television
news anchor announced. We are about to release the election
forecast of the Israeli television. We are taking some risks, but this is our
forecast, the major opposition party, the Likud headed by Menachem Begin,
has won the election. He defined it as a political turnover,
it was more than a mere turnover, it was a historical upheaval
that shook Israeli society. Ostensibly, there was a change of
government through election as sometimes occurs in democracies. But this particular change signals
the end of more than 50 years of exclusive hegemony of labor
movement over Israeli politics and many other realms of Israeli public life. Upheavals tend to take
society by surprise. But a closer look reveals the preliminary
indications that led to this change. The crisis of faith caused
by the Yom Kippur War gave rise to fierce
criticism of the government. That ultimately was translated into
a ballot box shortly after the war, the labor politicians that were
responsible for the war resigned. And were replaced by other
member of the labor party. But the new labor government
did not regain public confidence in its leadership. Economic hardship, terrorists attacks. Unclear policy toward
the settlement movement and the territories caused public
discontentment towards Rabin's government. New revelations of public
corruption among top labor figures further contributed to
the feeling of disillusionment. Few leading officials from
the labor establishment got involved in corrupt activities. Some of them were found
guilty of bribery and mismanagement which made the whole labor
establishment guilty by association. Suspicion against the Housing Minister
led him to commit suicide. The resignation of Rabin from office. Following an offense he committed against the law that forbade
Israelis to hold bank accounts abroad. Also lead to public dismay from
the conduct of labor leaders. All these issues contributed to
the diminishing power of the labor party. However, they can only partly account for
the upheaval. The central factor derived from the
resentment of Oriental Jews, Sephardi. Towards the Ashkenazi elite whom they
associated with the labor establishment. They were underrepresented in government
position and felt neglected and deprived as someone second class citizens,
alienated from society and culture. This feeling was an outcome
of the difficult absorption of Sepharadic Jews
during the first years of the state. The economic hardship suffered by
Oriental Jews and the sociocultural discrimination were particularly
stressed after the Six Days War. Many of them compared their
situation with the increased wealth of the Israeli Ashkenazi
middle class. Young Oriental Jews founded
the Black Panther movement in the underprivileged
neighborhood of Jerusalem as a social protest focused against
all sort of discrimination. In May 1971,
violent clashes erupted between protesters and the police and 74 members
of the organization were arrested. Golda Meir, Prime Minister at that time, expressed a deep disgust
towards the violence. Following a meeting with member
of the Black Panthers movement, she coined the expression,
they are not nice. This statement summed up
the labor leaders' utter incomprehension of the feeling
of the Sephardic Jewish public. Menachem Begin, the opposition leader for the decades exploited this feeling in
order to advance his own political agenda. He succeeded in winning over
the Sephardic communities by relating to them as
Israelis of equal worth. He accepted them as they were and did not
try to mold them into a new Israelis. Being the leader of the opposition
since the establishment of the state, he was also perceived as a victim
of the Ashkenazi hegemony. as representing those exploited and oppressed by Mapai labor establishment. This is how the public saw him and this is what gave the Likud
party its mass support since the late 70s and up to the present. During the 70s, the party managed to promote local politicians
of Sephardic origins. A strategy that ultimately enabled it
to form the government in 1977 and to dominate Israeli national
politic throughout the 80s. Nevertheless, the top leaders of the Likud
government, like Menachem Begin, Itzhak Shamir, Ariel Sharon and Binyamin
Netanyahu were of Ashkenazi origin. In general, since 1977 and
up to the end of the century, a two party system
dominated Israeli politics. The national block headed
by the Likud Party and its national religious
right-wing allies on one side. And the labor block and its social democratic and liberal
left-wing allies on the other side.
