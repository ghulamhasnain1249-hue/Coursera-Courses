In sum, the first two decades
were very stormy and eventful. The new state of Israel attempted
to achieve regional and international legitimacy. It tried to reinforce its identity
as a Jewish and democratic state, while absorbing an unprecedented
wave of mass immigration. Despite the impressive
absorption of new immigrants, most of whom were poor
refugees from Arab countries, fissures and tensions developed
within the Israeli societies. The country managed to
stabilize its economy and enlisted its veteran Jewish citizens in
the collective and national projects. The ongoing conflict with the Arab world
reaches a peak in the Sinai operation, which was a great military victory,
followed by a diplomatic setback. However, despite striving for a common,
young, modern, new Israeli identity, the population of the country
remained diverse and heterogenic. But as an enlisted society,
this diversity had no normative status and was seen as a temporary condition
until all the Jewish citizens of the country would be molded into
the new Israeli Sabra identity. Tragic events such as
the Kfar Qasim Massacre brought the problematic status of Arab
citizens of Israel to the forefront. It challenged the country to
deal with the complex and uneasy relations between the Jewish
majority and the Arab minority.
