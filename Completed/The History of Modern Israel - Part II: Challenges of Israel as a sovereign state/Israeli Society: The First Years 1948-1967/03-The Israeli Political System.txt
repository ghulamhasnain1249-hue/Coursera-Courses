[MUSIC] Like in most of the world's democracies,
Israel follows the pattern of a parliamentary democracy
based on separation of power. A legislative branch,
an executive branch, and an independent judicial branch. The citizens vote for
the Israeli parliament, the Knesset which is
the legislative branch. They elect their favorite
party out of many. If no single party receives ultimate
majority, the largest party has to form a coalition with other parties
in order to rule by forming a government. This government is approved by the vote of the majority of
the members of the Knesset. The government leads the executive branch
managing its different departments and various institutions. The members of the Knesset approve
the government's composition and monitor government activity. It is for these reasons that the government is
located at the mid of the Knesset hall. Under such a political
system the prime minister is the actual leader of the state. Heading the government. The Judicial Branch is
based on the court system. Judges are nominated by a professional and
a political committee. The court system is headed by
the Supreme Court of the country. [MUSIC] Beside these institutions, a representative head of the state is the president, who has a more symbolic
status than real political power. The president is also elected
by the members of the Knesset. [MUSIC]
