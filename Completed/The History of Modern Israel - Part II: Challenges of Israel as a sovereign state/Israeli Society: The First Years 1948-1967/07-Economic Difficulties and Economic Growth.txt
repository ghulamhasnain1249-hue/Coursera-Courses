Following the War of Independence, Israel
reached the verge of an economic collapse. The British financial infrastructure
was gone with the end of the mandate. The cost of the war was enormous and
mass immigration of impoverished and penniless newcomers who entered
the country further burden the budget. Under such difficult circumstances
the government issues austerity measures in the early 50's. People were allowed to get basic products
through government issued food stamps. Heavy tax was imposed on other goods. Food supply was restricted to
basic need of approximately 2,600 daily calorie per person. And other measure of controlled
consumption were imposed by the minister of rationing and supply. This system, which aimed to encourage
saving and austerity may have prevented an economic collapse but it also did not
encourage economic development and growth. Black market flourished and
official unsuccessfully tried to eliminate this phenomena by wasting time and
energy on road inspection and propaganda. Meanwhile, the government reached
a reparation agreement with West Germany, whereby Germany should pay money
to the state of Israel and the Holocaust survivors. Opponents of the agreement, headed by
Menachem Begin and other opposition parties accused Ben Gurion of selling
the Holocaust trauma for material gain. Many claimed that receiving payment
implies forgiving Germany. The opponents of the agreement held stormy
and sometimes even violent demonstration. After a bomb exploded in the Knesset,
the government even considered outlawing the extreme right wing party and the army
was put on alert for civilian uprising. Ultimately, the Israeli parliament
approved the agreement by a narrow margin. The fund from Germany played
a crucial role in stabilizing the economy of the young state. Many immigrants who had arrived from
Europe also received personal compensation from Germany and were thus able to
establish themselves financially. In general, during these decades, the
Israeli economy was highly state-centered, managed by government initiative,
rather than by market mechanism. It operated according to a political and national purpose defined
by the government. This strategy gave
priority to collective and public sector of the society
over private enterprise. Land ownership was mostly nationalized. State institution allocate funds and
public state-owned companies comprise the lion's share
of the economic activities. The private sector existed but its share
in the economic project was minor. The major financial source of the new
state were the German reparations, US annual aids, and
bonds purchased by American Jews. These sources enabled the government to
improve the economic infrastructure and the Israeli market started to
grow in an annual rate of 10%. The State defined agriculture
as a national priority. Between 1948 and 1953,
354 settlements were founded followed by additional
100 in the next decade. The area of cultivated land tripled, enabled Israel to reach agricultural
independence in the late 50s. When the country even started to
export agriculture product and technology overseas. Irrigation projects above all
the national water carrier brought water from the Sea
of Galilee to the south. Despite such project the state neither
established a single Arab settlement nor improved the infrastructure of
the existing Arab villages. Since the late 50s, and
throughout the 60s, the state of Israel focused on
industrial and urban development. It built two harbors in Ashdod and Eilat. bolstered the pharmaceutical,
chemical, electronical, military, and aerial industries. And encouraged the diamond industry,
which made the country a major center for processing and
larger diamond exporter in the world. The construction and
building industry grew significantly, employing more than 10% of the
working force. The government subsidize food and textile industry which helped
immigrants' absorption and employment. National ground, air and sea transportation increased during this decade. And the state or Histadrut owned
transportation companies, such as EGGED, ZIM or EL AL, increased in size and
enlarged their activities. This state-centered economy,
despite the economic growth it generate, and the relative welfare it
provided to the population was founded on a precarious ground. Military budgets were high, import surpassed export, thus leading
to a negative balance of payment. In the second half of the 60s,
the economy slowed down, and the country experienced
a severe recession, which only ended after the six
days war in the late 60s.
