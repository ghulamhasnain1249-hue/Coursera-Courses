In many cases of immigrants from Islamic countries, the patriarchal
family suffered acute disruption. The father who signified authority, power,
and knowledge in the Islamic countries of origin, often lose its
status unable to earn a living. And finding his normative world
irrelevant to the new surrounding. As newcomers continue to arrive, they were sent to development town and
villages, mainly border zone. Decentralization lay at
the root of this policy together with an attempt
populate remote places. Thus reinforcing Jewish
presence along the borders. The result was that many new immigrants
were forced to live at the periphery. Many of them grew increasingly embittered
and angry towards the authorities. And toward the veteran Israeli
establishment in general. Occasionally violence erupted. The most well-known incident occurred
in Wadi Salib in Haifa in 1959. When North African immigrants
protested against the neglectful and patronizing attitude of the Ashkenazi,
the Western-oriented establishment. The demonstrations were
suppressed by using great force. But the negative feeling, experienced
by the other Israel, the Sephardic Jews who lived
in hardship lingered on. The protests entered
Israeli consciousness. Exposing the social gap between citizens
from western origins - "Ashkenazim" - and those originating from North Africa and
other Arab countries - "Mizrahim". In retrospect, there is no doubt that in many cases the veteran Ashkenazi
establishment did tend to patronize and denigrate the Jews
who came from Arab countries. Indeed, feelings of alienation and
anger on the part of new immigrants transcended the context of
absorption in the 50s and the 60s. Ever since Ashkenazi-Sephardi
relations shaped politics, social and economic status, religious attitude and
cultural values and taste. Yet despite these shortcoming and
deficiencies, the impressive and unprecedented achievement of
immigrants absorption in the 50s and 60s cannot be overlooked. Israel in those decades succeeded in
absorbing about 1.5 million immigrants, providing most
of them with housing. And creating a new Israeli culture from
the great mix of languages and traditions. All these were achieved under difficult
condition of economic hardship, security threat and an on-going process of national
building of the newly conceived state.
