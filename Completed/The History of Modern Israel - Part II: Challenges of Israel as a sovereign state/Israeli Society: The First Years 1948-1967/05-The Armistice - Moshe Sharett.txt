The Armistice was important to both sides, as cleared by Moshe Sharett, then Foreign Minister of Israel: 

"It is obvious that this turn was due to these countries' defeat in the war and the fear of Israel spreading even further if no international blinds will be placed . However Israel was interested in a truce not only because of the delicacy and sensitivity of its international status, not only to eliminate the transience and tension of respite, but because the necessity - which day to day stressed more and more - to devote to the absorption process and to receive the newcomers, which began flowing in a dizzying rate. In the choice between a permanent peace and a truce without a date of completion, the Armistice was for the lesser of two evils to the Arabs and a temperance for Israel"
   

(Sharet, m.,In the Gate of Nations, 1958, p. 236)





The Kfar Qasim massacre court trial

In the Court trial of the soldiers who carried out the Kfar Qasim massacre judge Benjamin Halevy said:
"There are orders that are 'Manifestly illegal' in all circumstances and even if the command is given at the highest of commands. ... A signal recognition of 'Manifestly illegal commands' is to be as a black flag above the given command, as a warning saying: 'no!' not formal invalidity ...is what is Important here, but.... invalidity that is piercing the eye and rouses the heart against – this is how 'Manifestly illegal' is required dismiss a soldier's duty to comply and impose criminal responsibility for his actions"
(in: Rosenthal, r. (ed.), Kfar Qasim, hakibbutz hameuchad, 2000, p. 235).
