Indeed, the State of Israel may have
won the War of Independence, but security matters during the 50s and
60s were daunting. 6,000 members of the Jewish population
were killed in the war and the threat posed by Arab countries, and the
Palestinians to the state did not decline. Israelis believed that the armistice
agreement with the Arab states would put an end to violence between Jews and
Arabs. They hoped that the ceasefire border will
turn into recognized permanent borders and rejected any claim of dislocated
Palestinians to return to their borders. The Arabs viewed the agreement
as a temporary ceasefire and continue the confrontation,
blocking waterways. An economic boycott. Hostile propaganda and
refusal to recognize the new state. The Arabs insisted that the dislocated
Palestinian refugees had the right to return to their homes and demanded to have
their land, and property restored to them. The Arab states believed that
they have the obligation to assist Palestinian refugees to return. Indeed, infiltration by refugees
into Israeli territories began immediately after the end of the war. From 1949 to 1956, many shooting incidents
took place between Arab infiltrators and Israeli defense forces, making the
border insecure and vulnerable. Many Israeli soldiers and civilians were
attacked, and killed near the borders. Israel react by forming special
unit that exercise retaliatory operations against Arab villagers
across the Egyptian and Jordanian border, which were viewed
the bases for the infiltration. Some of these operations caused
civilian casualties like the one Qibya,
Jordan where commando unit under the command of Ariel Sharon
killed 69 Arab residents. The Israel media aligned itself
with Ben-Gurion's claim that it was not the the IDF soldiers that
carried out this action, but rather angry residents of
Israel peripheral community. On the other hand, a few voices in
Israel condemned this kind of collective punishment stating  that: "Even in our
gloomy world, the world of the jungle, one recoils from the indiscriminate murder
of women, children and non-combatants." Unfortunately, this dilemma of
retaliation against the enemy is a basic dilemma that
Israeli society faces til today. The admiration for the paratroopers
that executed the retaliatory raid created a new ethos
of patriotic militarism. The commanders, soldiers and
officers were seen as strong and brave and many young Israelis
looked up to them, as role models. Tension between Israel and
its neighbors increased in the mid-50s. Terrorist activity by Palestinian
infiltrators continued. Syria's struggles for
control of water resources ended with a shooting incident in the north and
Egypt's arm deal with the Soviets reinforced
the feeling of threat in Israel. The nationalization of the Suez Canal that
operated under control of Britain and France by Egyptian President Gamal Abdel
Nasser prevented Israeli ships from passing through it, and cut off a major
sea passage between Israel, and Asia. A secret agreement was signed
between Britain, France and Israel to join forces against Egypt. Israel's role was to
conquer the Sinai Desert. France signed an arms deal with
the country and thereby, gradually becoming its major
arms supplier by the mid-60s. The Sinai operation began in October 29th,
1956. In three days battle, the Israeli
army defeated the Egyptian forces and succeeded in taking the Gaza Strip and
most of the Sinai Peninsula. This military victory resumed
public confidence towards the IDF and great admiration for its fighter. The Israeli military victory did
not translate into a significant diplomatic achievement. The attack on Egypt caused great anger
on part of the two super powers, the USA and the Soviet Union. They both condemned the war and
demanded of Israel, and its ally to withdraw immediately
from the occupied territories. Israel capitulated to
the diplomatic pressure. It pulled back its forces from Sinai and
returned to the international border. Yet, Israel gained certain achievements. First, United Nations forces were
positioned in Sinai and Gaza Strip. And thus, maintained security and prevented any further infiltration
to Israel from these areas. Second, the Straits of Tiran
were reopened for Israeli ships. Overall, in this operation, Israel demonstrated its military power
both to itself and to the world. At the eve of the Sinai operation,
one of the most tragic and shameful events in the history
of the country took place. Namely, the massacre in the Arab
village Kfar Qasim. As part of the preparation to the war, a curfew was declared on certain
Arab villages in the country. In Kfar-Qasim,
the information did not reach those people working in the fields
surrounding the village. When they came back to the village, unaware of the curfew,
they were shot to death. Civilians were shot,
men, women and children. A few days after the massacre, the officers who gave the order to
shoot were arrested and put to trial, which became a central issue In
the Israeli public discourse. Few officers were given prison
sentence of 7 to 15 years and were demoted to the rank of private. Two years later, presidential
clemency was granted to those who had carried out the massacre and
they were released from jail. The harsh punishment on the one hand and
the quick clemency on the other demonstrated the different
attitudes toward this atrocity. While the judiciary system clearly
demarked the boundaries between what is permissible and what is not. The political and military system
stepped in and minimized the punishment. Til this day, the massacre is viewed
as a blemish on the history of Israel. It was perceived as an exception,
but also as sign of warning. The ruling against those accused in the
affair clarified in the term, a clearly illegal order. Meaning, an order that runs
counter to human ethics, which the IDF soldiers and
commanders must refuse to obey.
