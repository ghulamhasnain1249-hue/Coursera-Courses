During its first two decades, Israel's status
as a sovereign state was insecure and dubious. Its Arab neighboring states did
not recognize its legitimacy. No superpowers support and
backed its interest, the Soviets moved to support
the Arab states in the region. And the American,
despite some financial help, did not commit themselves to securing
Israel's precarious existence. The great diplomatic and
military ally of the country was France, who supplied the IDF with modern weapons,
and even helped building
Israel's nuclear capacity. Despite regional isolation,
the state of Israel established diplomatic relations with many
countries in Africa and Asia. And provided them with technological and
even military assistance. In term of domestic policy, Mapai and the
labor block controlled Israeli politics, most of the time under
the leadership of David Ben-Gurion. He later resigned from politics
following the calamity, which came to be known
as the Lavon Affair. Israeli intelligence operated
the sale of Egyptian jewels, aiming to disrupt the relations
between Egypt and the west. Member of the cell were caught. And the Defense Minister Lavon resigned, albeit claiming that it was not he
who had initiated the operation. The affair took on broad proportions, causing a serious crisis
in the ruling party Mapai. It ultimately led to the resignation
of Ben-Gurion from his position as Prime Minister and
Defense Minister in 1963. He was replaced by
the Finance Minister Levi Eshkol.
