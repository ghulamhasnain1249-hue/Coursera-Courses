The high-tech revolution that
occurred at the turn of the century has a huge positive impact on Israel. New developments in advanced technology
required professional manpower which gave Israel's economy
an enormous momentum. Computer Science, Communication,
Electronics, Information Industry, Medical Equipment, and other high
tech enterprises  pushed the Israeli economy forward and become the catalyst
of the country's economic growth. Thousands of high tech projects were
launched and spread all over the country. And many new high tech centers
grew almost over night, many of these projects succeeded
in the global competitive market, which required new technological and
creative atmosphere. In the last generation Israel, despite its small size,
became a start up nation. The high tech industry turned to be
the major generator of the country's economic growth and the main branch of its
export by spreading sophisticated and creative knowledge to the global market. The Israeli high tech industry has many
faces and forms of manifestation. Many small enterprises that few
Israeli high tech engineer started had developed to a certain level and
then made an exit by being sold to a giant hyper
global mainly American corporations. Other Israeli high tech firms
function as a research center of a giant global companies. Still others keep their independent nature
by developing a unique product or service. Some high tech giants such as
Intel open branches, factories, and offices, in Israel due to a favorable investment incentives
they received from the government. Many of the high tech initiative
began with creative individuals who grew up in a technological
environment from an early age, whether in high school or
while serving in the army. Then, they further developed
the skills by studying in Institute of Higher Educations such as Technion,
the Weizmann Institute, University in other Israeli University
than research center. It is hard to clarify what made
Israel a start up nation, but there are some tentative explanation. The existence of a large segment of
the Israeli population which values intellectual development and
appreciates culture of learning, provided the engine for
developing the cyber industry. An atmosphere of innovation that
encouraged creative thinking, unconventional experimentation,
breaking routines and taking intellectual risks
certainly played a role. Modern management methods and
cooperative working environments encourage teamwork that contribute to
Israeli success in the high tech industry. Whatever the explanation may be,
Israel placed itself at the forefront of the global high tech frontier industry,
and it contributes enormously
to the growth and prosperity of the Israeli economy and
society at large. However, this new economic development has
some negative impact in several fields. A significant gap has been widening
between both in the high tech industry, and those will remain behind in
traditional low tech industries. This gap is manifested in salaries,
working condition, lifestyle, and prestige. The high tech industry is competitive,
flexible, unpredictable, and dynamic. It prefers young, innovative,
workers over older and more experienced. As a result, people at the age of 40 and
over seem obsolete. In terms of gender differences,
the industry is male-dominated, to require the working load of many hours that is defined by labor expert
as a new form of slavery. Within a global high tech market,
achievement and success of Israeli firm can be at the
expense of the Israeli national interest. Sometime, it seems that emphasis
on short term benefit of the high tech lead to diversion
of other field of the economy. For example, Israel is a start
up nation with a third world public transportation system. The country lack an underground
in cities and urban centers. The road infrastructure cannot contain
the traffic being condensed and dangerous. It leads, among other factor,
to high rates of fatal car accidents. Nonetheless, it is undeniable
that high tech as a new economic development increased the country's
economics grow in the 21st century. An annual rate of 3 to 7% of growth
was common during the last 15 years. This overall economic growth
unrelated to the high tech expansion is unevenly distributed. A small network of well established,
and interconnected families control most of the market which
suffers for stifling centralization. Most of the capital is in the hands
of private monopolies headed by rich tycoons who abuse their power
by influencing politicians and civil officials that are supposed to
regulate and restrict their power. Driven by greed and
obsessed with power and control, this group of multi-millionaires
gets more and more charters and contracts to develop the natural and
financial resources of the state. The cases of the natural gas reservoir or the Dead Sea chemicals are the most
explicit examples of such a phenomenon. Some would argue, but by controlling
the politician through corruption and promises, these millionaires in
fact exploit the national resource that belong to the Israeli public for
the private profit seeking interest.
