Indeed Israel's political system experiences severe problems of
governmentability in the 21st century. Following the assassination of Prime
Minister Rabin, the Labor Party headed by Shimon Peres continued to govern for
a few months until the election of 1996. In this election, the Labour lost
to the Likud party, and Benjamin Natnahayu became
the new prime minister. In 1999 Natnahayu lost to Ehud Barak, the new Labor leader, who headed
the Labor government till 2001. When he lost to the Likud
leader Ariel Sharon. Sharon headed two consecutive
governments from 2001 until 2006, when he suffered cerebral hemorrhage and
went into coma. As Prime Minister,
he reached unprecedented popularity, which enabled him to found a new political
party aimed at the political center and attracting members both from Likud,
and Labor. This party, Kadima, won the election
in 2006, under the leadership of Ehud Olmert,
who was the Prime Minister until 2009. In the next election, the Likud was able
to form a coalition under the leadership of Benjamin Netanyahu, who narrowly
won the next consecutive elections in 2013 and 2015 thus continues to serve as Israel's Prime Minister
heading a right-wing government. Elections are supposed to take place in
Israel every four years but since 1996, Israel went through seven election
campaigns an average of two and a half years from one campaign to
the other which underscores the problem of govermentability and
lack of governmental stability. Beyond this pendulum movement in
hyperpolitics, we can identify certain long-term trends that transform
the Israeli democratic system. Overall, these trends seriously
challenge the status and prestige of Israeli
politics. fragmentation - In 1977, there was one hegemonic party, which controlled the political system for
decades, without real threat to its reign. As mentioned in previous lessons, since 1977 two large block Likud
on the right and labor on the left dominate Israeli party system making
it practically a two party system. They compete over the Israeli
electorate who for most of the time since 1977
gave advantage to the Likud. But in times of stalemate
between the two blocks, they were able to form a bipartisan
national union government, since together they control
the vast majority of The Knesset. Since 2000, the party system has
became increasingly fragmented the parties reflect Israeli society
as a sectorial and segmented society. The parties represent various
interest groups, ethnic and national minorities, religious identity,
and cultural lifestyle. Thus even the largest parties
have become sectorial, sectional and smaller in their size. Today, even if Likud an Labor
join forces, they would not constitute
a majority of the Knesset. In such, a fragmented political system,
it is hard for the government to sustain control for
the entire term. Israel therefore experiences
an ongoing crisis of governmentability. Lack of Ideological Agenda. Like many other parts of
the democratic world, Israeli democracy suffers from
blurring of ideological differences. And evading serious public debate over
core issues and well-articulated agendas. The politicians prefer to
remain obscure in the programs, avoid expressing their agenda, which many
of them hardly have, and concentrate on images, media appearance, association
with celebrities, and populism. They are caught up in identity politics trying to ingratiate themselves with
the potential voters and gain popularity. So they hardly express their
opinions on core issues. They reduce discussion on political
agendas to simplistic slogans and disseminate populist promises
which they forget once in office. As a result, a crisis of confidence is
mounting between the general public and the supposed representatives who
often fail to act in accordance With their election time platforms and
promises. Corruption, the most alarming deficiency
of Israeli politics in the last years has been the apparent corruption that
affects so many office holders in Israel. Some might claim that this
has always been the case. Power corrupts and Israeli politicians
are not immune of this disease. In the past, without the scrutiny
of investigating journalism, it was easy to conceal this
corruption from the public eye. you to various changes some cultural some
technological the rules of public conduct have changed it is more difficult in the
21st century to keep corruption concealed from the media especially the social
media and avoiding public attention. This behavior leads to public
abhorrence of politicians and mistrust in the political system, thereby weakening the democratic
foundations of the country. Nonetheless, whenever the legal system
succeeds in fighting corruption, it exposes the unethical and illegal behavior
of many people in position of power. These victories demonstrate the
perseverance of the judicial system, in protecting the Democratic system by
prosecuting corrupted politicians and office holders. Israel today has a former president serving
a prison time for rape and sexual abuse. The former minister of finance is in jail
for bribery, and the former Prime Minister who was convicted of serious
allegations of criminal activities. In addition, high-ranking police officers are facing accusations
of sexual harassment. Affiliation with organized crime and
other felonies. A significant number of
top officials underwent police interrogation under
warning in the last decade. Many office holders and
politicians were found guilty and served jail terms like the last criminal. Many others escaped legal
procedures because of weak incriminating evidence despite
public conviction of their guilt. One of the criminals,
after serving in-prison term, even dared to return to politics to become
the head of the religious party and now serve as a minister in the government. Most of the felonies and crimes belong to the spheres of financial
corruption and sexual harassment. Such a phenomena occasionally
dominates the news headlines whereby significantly
degrading the status and prestige of Israeli politician and
top public office holder. Some would claim that such a political
mismanagement cause many Israelis to despise politics and
avoid entering this sphere. People of high moral and
intellectual quality avoid politics and leave the field to mediocre
if not second rate persons. Such a trend damages the Israeli civil
society and facilitates business tycoons and their lobbyists in taking control
over any position of power in the country. others, are reluctant in
drawing such harsh conclusions. and claim instead
that the few who went astray mar the reputation of an otherwise decent and
functional governmental system. In any case,
the politics of anti-politics became part of the Israeli public
discourse in the last decades. Occasionally, it promotes reform and
protest aiming to transform the system. Yet in most cases,
it leads to cynicism, despair and disengagement from the democratic
system as a whole. One of the positive outcomes of
the crisis in Israeli politics is the emergence of many NGOs. It contributes to the mobilization
of Israeli civic society toward the various economic,
social, and political goals.
