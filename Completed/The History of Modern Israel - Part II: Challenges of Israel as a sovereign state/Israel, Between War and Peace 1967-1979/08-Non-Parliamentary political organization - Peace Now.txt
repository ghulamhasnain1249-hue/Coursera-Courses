One of the most well-known rivals
to the settlement movement is the non-parliamentary movement, Peace Now. This movement was established
in 1978 following the request submitted by
350 reserve officers to Prime Minister Menachem Begin to continue
on the path of peace with Egypt. It later spearheaded the resistance
to Israeli settlement policy in the occupied territories. According to Peace Now, the continuation
of settlement projects would enforce an everlasting obstacle
to any future reconciliation and peace initiative between Israel and
the Palestinians. The movement organized mass demonstrations
and rallies in order to convince the public to support peace initiatives
on the basis of land for peace. Peace Now provided public support to
any government that would endorse a two-state solution and a willingness
to withdraw from occupied territories. It played an important role in periods of
negotiations between Israel and its enemy. However, in the last 20 years, the
movement has lost most of its influence due to the stagnation of the peace process
and the escalating in the conflict. Another prominent dissenting
voice was that of Abie Nathan. Nathan, a journalist and
well-known Bohemian figure, operated a pirat broadcasting station
called the Voice of Peace for almost two decade since 1975. It broadcasted from the Mediterranean Sea
beyond Israeli territorial waters, featuring music and
Jewish-Arabic dialogue, which was a rare phenomenon
during those years. Though anecdotal in its impact, the Voice
of Peace is considered to have heralded Israel's willingness to make concessions
in exchange for the hope of peace.
