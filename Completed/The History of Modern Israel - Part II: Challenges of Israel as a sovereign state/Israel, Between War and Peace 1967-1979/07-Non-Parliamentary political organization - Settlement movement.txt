As mentioned earlier, the diverse opinions
regarding the fate of the territories led to a lack of clear approved
settlement policy by the government. However in 1974, an energetic settlement movement was established,
known as Gush Emunim. The movement, which followed
the guidance of Rabbi Kook who declared that all the land belonged
to the Jews as a biblical promise, was infused with religious,
messianic zeal. Taking advantage of the lack
of clear settlement policy, it established fact on the ground. The movement enjoyed active support
from the right wing parties, and passive support from
most of the other parties. The movement began its settlement
project in the mountains of Samaria. Following failed attempts
to settle illegally, it finally got authorization for
temporary settlement. Later on, they got government authorization to
establish a permanent settlement. The settlement efforts of Gush Emunim and
its right wing supporters received significant backing after
the 1977 victory of the Likud Party. The settlement movement focused its
efforts on building Jewish villages and towns on the eastern edge of Samaria and
the western margins of the West Bank. They aimed to build new
settlement on what they perceived as ancient Jewish biblical places. Their goal was to prevent any
possible territorial withdrawal from the area they believed belonged
to Israel by biblical promise. Beside this ideological movement, many settlements were established in
the West Bank and Gaza out of expediency. Since 1977,
the government policy encouraged people to settle there by
providing cheap land and housing. This policy attracted many Israelis who
wished to improve their housing condition, and bought cheap lands and real estate in
the territories beyond the green line. Many Israelis settled in the territories out of pragmatic consideration
rather than ideology. Thus, creating the Jewish towns and villages in the midst of
the Palestinian population. By practically supporting the Gush Emunim
effort to settle the West Bank and Gaza Strip, it seems that
the Israeli government intends to keep the occupied territories
under its control. As of 2005,
230,000 settlers were living in these territories in 145 settlements in
the West Bank and 17 in the Gaza Strip. The settlements in Gaza
were evacuated in 2005, but the settlements in the West Bank
steadily increased in size and number. Today, almost half a million
Jews are living beyond the green line borders in Jerusalem and
in the West Bank. The settlement project is
not recognized by the world, which views them as a violation
of international law. Despite the impressive success of
the movement within Israeli society, the issue of the settlement beyond
the green line is still under fierce dispute between its supporters and
opponents. For many in the country and beyond, it has become the most contentious
issue in Israeli politics today.
