The Consuming anxiety (Michael Oren) 
"The masses of civilians... were  enveloped by anxiety. Thousands of people across the country dealt with urgency in digging trenches, building shelters and filling sandbags. Special emphasis has been placed on Jerusalem, where schools were to serve as shelters against bombardments, and practice against air raids were made daily routines... An emergency operation of blood donating was declared. Urgent request to launch surgeons -- 'in light of the difficult conditions they should be people with fitness and experience' — was submitted to the Red Cross, and additional plasma [blood] sets were booked overseas. Special committees were established to collect foodstuffs, filling the role of employees recruited to the front and children evacuation to Europe.  More than 14,000 beds in hospitals were prepared for the wounded,  and drugs to neutralize the veil of gas victims were stored. The only bright spot in these preparations was the unprecedented emotional empathy that flooded the country from around the Jewish world. Volunteers from abroad arrived in numbers beyond the capacity of the country... And donations rose significantly to all predictions. In New York and London demonstrations of mass support were held... These gestures eased but slightly the sense of impending disaster, the deserted Jews to yet another Holocaust. " 

 (Oren, m. Six Days of War, Dvir, 2004. p.172-173
