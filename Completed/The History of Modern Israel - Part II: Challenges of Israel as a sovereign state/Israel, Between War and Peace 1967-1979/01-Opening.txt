From the late 60s until the late 70s,
Israeli society experienced 2 major wars that had a huge impact on life in the
country, as well as 1 peace agreement that altered the status of Israel in the area
and reinvigorated hope for a better future. The Six-Day War ended with
an unimaginable victory over three Arab states. It led to a geographical and demographical changes as well as
political and military challenges. By contrast, the surprising
outbreak of the Yom Kippur War endanger the very
existence of the country. Though it ended with a significant
military achievement, the trauma of the surprising attack and
the heavy toll that Israeli society paid in the war resulted in harsh
criticism towards the leadership and the disillusionment that shook
Israeli basic assumptions and self-perception to the core. The war led to a significant protest
against the political leadership of the state. The peace with Egypt at the end
of the decade, albeit cold and controversial led to Israeli
withdrawal from Sinai and strengthened the legitimacy
of the state in the region.
