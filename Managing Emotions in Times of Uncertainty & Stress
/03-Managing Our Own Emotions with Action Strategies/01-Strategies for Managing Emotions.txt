There are infinite kinds of strategies, from exercising or taking a walk, to drawing or listening to music, connecting with friends, or snuggling with a pet. What works for one person may not work for another. Our temperament, personality, and personal preferences all influence the strategies we use. Context or location also affects which strategies we choose and use; a strategy that works at home (like taking a nap) may not be possible at work. 

Culture also plays a role. Different cultures value some qualities over others. Some cultures provide more opportunities for community and social support, while others value self-reliance. As you consider strategies that are most helpful for you, you may choose to follow your cultural values, or purposefully go against them.

Building a toolkit of regulation strategies is a lifelong process. As we grow and face life’s challenges, we will continue to learn and try new strategies. 

In the next few sections, we’re going to review strategies that research has shown to be helpful. This week focuses on both action and thought strategies.
