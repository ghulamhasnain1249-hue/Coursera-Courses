At the Yale Center for
Emotional Intelligence, we talk a lot about why emotions matter and there are
five critical reasons. The first is attention,
memory, and learning. Let's face it. Over the
last six or so months, we've experienced a lot
of unpleasant feelings. From anxiety, to
stress, to frustration, to anger, to boredom,
to loneliness. What we know from
research is that for both children and adults, when we experience a lot of
strong, unpleasant emotions, we go into survival mode-- not teaching and learning mode. As a matter of fact, what we know is that we go
into that fight, flight, or freeze response. Maybe you can relate to that. We don't go into, "Oh, let me
learn something new" mode. As a matter of fact,
the areas of our brain responsible for
taking in information literally get cut off when we're experiencing these
high levels of stress. Importantly, moderate
levels of stress are okay. That stress associated with trying to learn something new, or playing a new game, that's all the good stress. Unfortunately, what I'm talking about here is that bad stress. Many of our students are
going to be coming back to school looking distracted, being overwhelmed and frustrated, and it may look like they
have even learning problems. But the point I want
to make here is that it just might be that they've
had so much stress that hasn't been managed that
it's manifesting itself in, what appears to be, a
learning difference. Also, what's really cool is that other emotions are very
helpful for learning. By way of example, when we experience inspiration
and curiosity, literally the areas of our brain responsible for thriving
and surviving light up. What's the message here?
I think there are three. The first is, that we
have to manage stress (our own and our
students') effectively. The second is that we have to create learning environments for both adults and children where we feel a sense
of safety and security, and connectedness,
and belongingness. Third, we have to create
as many opportunities as possible for ourselves, and for our students,
to experience that inspiration and curiosity.
