The second way emotions matter
is with decision making. We like to think of ourselves as rational
beings that were in total control over our judgments and choices in life. But the truth is how we feel very much
influences our choices and decisions. Take for example, you've had a really
rough morning, you didn't sleep well. You're kind of irritable, maybe had a
fight at home, and then you're at work and someone asks you to do something and
you're like, "No, I can't do that." Or they ask your opinion about
something and you say something like, "I don't think it's that great." What we do not realize is that that
choice or that judgment in that moment, likely had something to do
with how we felt that morning. This happens with positive emotions,
it happens with negative emotions. So for example, when we're in a very
sad mood, we tend to look at a task and we're like "It's going to be really
difficult". When we're in a very positive mood, we're like "Not a problem. That's easy." One way to get more skilled in this area
is to just check in with your feelings. So in between work and
home or home and work, you just take that moment and pause and
just say: "How am I feeling right now?" And what we know from research is that
when we attribute are feeling to its actual cause, it's less likely to have influence over
the subsequent choice or decision.
