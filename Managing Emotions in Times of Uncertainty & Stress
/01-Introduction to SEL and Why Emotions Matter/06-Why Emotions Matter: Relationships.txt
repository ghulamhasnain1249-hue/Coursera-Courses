The third reason
why emotions matter is that they influence to
quality of our relationships. In our Center, we like to
talk about emotions as signals to approach or avoid. This occurs for our own
experience of emotion. You ever wake up and
you're frustrated or overwhelmed and you're like, "I don't really want to
engage with people today," other days you wake
up and the sun is shining and you're having
just a pleasant mood, you're like, "I want to
talk to everybody I know." Also, what's interesting
about emotions is that we're observing the
world around us every day. We're noticing people's
facial expressions, body language, vocal tones. What we know from
research is that people who display a lot of negative emotions,
they're sending signals. What are they saying?
They're saying "Stay away, I may not respect you. I don't really want
to engage with you." On the other hand,
people who display more pleasant emotions, what are they saying?
"I'm here for you. Yeah, come talk! I'm approachable." Importantly, when it
comes to our students, they may be displaying a lot
of negative emotions because of their difficult circumstances over the last couple of months. They may act out, they may look overwhelmed and they may be anxious. And maybe they're behaving in ways that
are uncomfortable to us. But my hope is that you'll see that as a signal to approach. Because what's
probably underneath that behavior is a
need that is not met. Lastly, what I'd like to offer
you is a little exercise. Over the next couple of weeks, take a moment and just think about the different
people that you work with, and write their names down, and just jot down the first feeling that comes
to mind around that person. Ask yourself, does
that feeling influence the way I treat that person, the way I greet that person, the way I interact
with that person? What that exercise can
do is it can just build greater self-awareness about the quality of
our relationships.
